## Problem

As in the previous card, but each hat is **red, green or blue**. What strategy saves the most prisoners for sure, and how many?

## Solution

Code red $=0$, green $=1$, blue $=2$.

1. The first prisoner announces the colour coded by
$$s = \sum_{j \ne 0} h_j \bmod 3.$$
2. Prisoner $i$ knows $s$ and every other $h_j$ ($j\neq 0,i$), from sight and earlier correct guesses. The three candidates $x, x+1, x+2$ have different remainders mod 3, so exactly one fits:
$$h_i = \Big(s - \sum_{j\ne 0,i} h_j\Big) \bmod 3.$$
3. **99** saved for sure; the first prisoner survives with probability $1/3$.

The same works for any number of colours $k$ using mod $k$.

**Book note.** Page 24 lists the colours as red, blue and white, then codes red, green and blue. It's the same strategy either way.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Try to split colours into two groups and reuse parity.

**Why it fails.** One bit can't single out one of three colours.

**The trick.** A **parity bit** is just a **checksum mod 2**. With $k$ colours, broadcast a checksum mod $k$; the first speaker can say one of $k$ colours, which is exactly enough room.
