## Problem

You need to send a document to a colleague in Greenwich. The courier loses anything in an unlocked box (including keys or locks placed inside). Each of you has a padlock with a single key that never leaves its owner. How do you get the document to your colleague securely?

## Solution

1. You put the document in the box, lock it with **your** lock, send it.
2. Your colleague can't open it. He adds **his** lock and sends it back.
3. You remove **your** lock (his stays on) and send it again.
4. He removes his own lock and takes the document.

The box is locked on every trip, and no key ever travels.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Find a way to get your key, or his, across safely.

**Why it fails.** The problem rules that out: anything not locked is lost.

**The trick.** Locks *commute*: lock A on, lock B on, lock A off leaves lock B on. This is the idea behind Shamir's three-pass protocol in cryptography.
