## Problem

A drunk man stands at the 17th metre of a 100-metre bridge. Each step he moves forward or backward one metre with probability $\frac12$. What is the probability he reaches the far end (metre 100) before the start (metre 0)? What is the expected number of steps until he reaches either end?

## Solution

1. $S_n$ = position, a symmetric random walk: $S_n$ and $S_n^2 - n$ are martingales ($E[(S+X)^2 - (n+1)] = S^2 + 1 - n - 1$).
2. $N$ = first time at 0 or 100. Optional stopping on $S_n$, with $p = P(S_N = 100)$:
$$E[S_N] = 100p = S_0 = 17 \;\Rightarrow\; p = 0.17.$$
3. Optional stopping on $S_n^2 - n$:
$$\begin{aligned}E[S_N^2] - E[N] &= 17^2\\ 0.17\cdot100^2 - E[N] &= 289\\ E[N] &= 1700 - 289 = 1411.\end{aligned}$$
4. In general, starting $a$ from one end and $b$ from the other: $P = \frac{a}{a+b}$ (for the far end) and $E[N] = ab$; here $17\cdot83 = 1411$.

**Book note.** The book's text gives $E[N] = 1441$, an arithmetic slip; $17\times 83 = 1411$ (its own formula $E[N] = \alpha\beta$ gives 1411).

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Set up 99 linear equations for the Markov chain.

**Why it fails.** Correct but long.

**The trick.** A fair game stays fair when you stop at a reasonable time. Position gives the probability; position² minus time gives the duration. Find a martingale that contains the quantity you want.
