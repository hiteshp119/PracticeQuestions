## Problem

Three bags: apples, oranges, and a mix. The labels "apple", "orange" and "mix" are on them, but **every** label is wrong. What's the fewest fruits you must take out to identify all three bags?

## Solution

1. Draw one fruit from the bag labelled **mix**. That bag isn't the mix, so it's pure; say you draw an orange. Then it's the **orange** bag.
2. The bag labelled **apple** isn't apple, and orange is taken, so it's the **mix**.
3. The bag labelled **orange** is therefore **apple**.

(Draw an apple instead and swap the roles.) One fruit suffices, and zero obviously doesn't.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Draw from the "apple" bag. An orange means it's oranges or mix, so it's still ambiguous.

**Why it fails.** The "apple" and "orange" labels are symmetric, and each leaves two possibilities.

**The trick.** Pick the **unique, asymmetric** option: the "mix" label is the one bag guaranteed to be pure, so a single draw fully identifies it.
