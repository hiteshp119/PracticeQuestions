## Problem

You're given 98 distinct integers from $\{1,\dots,100\}$. What's a good way to find the two that are missing?

## Solution

Let the missing numbers be $x, y$ and the given ones $z_1,\dots,z_{98}$.

1. $S_1 = \sum_{n=1}^{100} n = 5050 \Rightarrow x + y = 5050 - \sum z_i =: a$.
2. $S_2 = \sum_{n=1}^{100} n^2 = \frac{100\cdot101\cdot201}{6} = 338350 \Rightarrow x^2 + y^2 = 338350 - \sum z_i^2 =: b$.
3. Then $2xy = a^2 - b$, so $(x-y)^2 = 2b - a^2$:
$$x, y = \frac{a \pm \sqrt{2b - a^2}}{2}.$$

One pass over the data: $O(n)$ time, $O(1)$ memory.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Sort and scan ($O(n\log n)$), or keep a 100-entry checklist ($O(n)$ memory).

**Why it fails.** Both work, but the interviewer is looking for constant memory.

**The trick.** Each unknown needs an equation, and **power sums** supply them: $k$ missing numbers need sums of powers $1,\dots,k$. With one missing number, the sum (or an XOR of everything) is enough.
