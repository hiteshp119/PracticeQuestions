## Problem

Define a (standard) Brownian motion and list its important properties.

## Solution

**Definition.** A process $W_t$, $t\ge0$, with
1. $W_0 = 0$;
2. independent increments: $W_{t_1} - W_{t_0},\dots,W_{t_n} - W_{t_{n-1}}$ independent for $t_0<\dots<t_n$;
3. $W_t - W_s\sim N(0,\,t-s)$ for $s<t$;
4. continuous paths.

**Properties.**
- $W_t\sim N(0,t)$; $\operatorname{Cov}(W_s, W_t) = \min(s,t)$.
- Markov, and a martingale: $E[W_{t+s}\mid\mathcal F_t] = W_t$.
- Paths are continuous but nowhere differentiable; quadratic variation over $[0,t]$ is $t$ ("$(dW)^2 = dt$").
- Scaling: $\frac1{\sqrt c}W_{ct}$ is again a Brownian motion.

**Martingales.**
1. $W_t^2 - t$: $E[W_{t+s}^2\mid\mathcal F_t] = W_t^2 + s$.
2. $Z_t = \exp\big(\lambda W_t - \frac12\lambda^2t\big)$: $E[e^{\lambda(W_{t+s} - W_t)}] = e^{\lambda^2s/2}$ (the normal MGF) cancels the drift term.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "A random walk with small steps."

**Why it fails.** True as a limit, but interviews want the precise definition, especially $W_0 = 0$ and *independent, normal* increments.

**The trick.** Remember it as "the continuous-time fair coin game": independence + variance proportional to time. The two extra martingales are just $W$'s square and exponential corrected for their drift.
