## Problem

You can price a European call either with constant volatility 30%, or by drawing the volatility from a distribution with mean 30%. Which price is higher?

## Solution

1. Vega: $\frac{\partial c}{\partial\sigma} = SN'(d_1)\sqrt T > 0$.
2. Volga (vega's sensitivity to $\sigma$), using $\frac{\partial d_1}{\partial\sigma} = -\frac{d_2}{\sigma}$:
$$\frac{\partial^2c}{\partial\sigma^2} = SN'(d_1)\sqrt T\cdot\Big(-d_1\frac{\partial d_1}{\partial\sigma}\Big) = \text{vega}\cdot\frac{d_1d_2}{\sigma}.$$
3. **Most options:** OTM ($d_1, d_2<0$) or ITM ($d_1,d_2>0$) have $d_1d_2>0$: $c$ is convex in $\sigma$, so by Jensen
$$E[c(\sigma)]\ \ge\ c(E[\sigma]):$$
random vol is more expensive.
4. **Exception:** near the money, $d_1 = d_2 + \sigma\sqrt T$ can be $>0$ while $d_2<0$: $c$ is concave there, and constant vol can give the higher price.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "More randomness, more option value: random vol is always dearer."

**Why it fails.** That needs convexity in $\sigma$, which fails close to the money (ATM price is almost **linear** in $\sigma$: $0.4\sigma S\sqrt T$, card 6.14).

**The trick.** Jensen's inequality questions are convexity questions. Compute the second derivative and check its sign in every region.
