## Problem

A non-dividend stock follows geometric Brownian motion (volatility $\sigma$, rate $r$). What is the value today of a contract that pays $1/S_T$ at maturity $T$?

## Solution

1. Under $\mathbb Q$: $\ln S_T = \ln S + (r - \frac12\sigma^2)T + \sigma W_T$, so
$$\ln\frac1{S_T}\sim N\Big(-\ln S - \big(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\big)T,\ \sigma^2T\Big).$$
2. Lognormal mean $E[e^X] = e^{\mu + \frac12 s^2}$:
$$\begin{aligned}E\Big[\frac1{S_T}\Big] &= \frac1S\exp\Big(-rT + \tfrac12\sigma^2T + \tfrac12\sigma^2T\Big)\\ &= \frac1Se^{(\sigma^2 - r)T}.\end{aligned}$$
3. Discount:
$$V = e^{-rT}E\Big[\frac1{S_T}\Big] = \frac1S\,e^{(\sigma^2-2r)T}.$$

**Itô check.** $V = 1/S$: $dV = -\frac{dS}{S^2} + \frac{(dS)^2}{S^3} = V\big((\sigma^2 - r)dt - \sigma\,dW\big)$, a GBM with drift $\sigma^2 - r$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** $1/E[S_T] = \frac1Se^{-rT}$, then discount: $\frac1Se^{-2rT}$.

**Why it fails.** $1/x$ is convex, so Jensen: $E[1/S_T] > 1/E[S_T]$. The gap is the $e^{\sigma^2T}$ factor.

**The trick.** Powers of a lognormal are lognormal: $S_T^n$ has mean $S^ne^{(nr + \frac12n(n-1)\sigma^2)T}$; set $n = -1$.
