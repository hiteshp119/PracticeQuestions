## Problem

Let $X\sim N(0,1)$. Find $E[X \mid X>0]$.

## Solution

1. Definition:
$$E[X\mid X>0] = \frac{\int_0^\infty x\,\varphi(x)\,dx}{P(X>0)},$$
$$\varphi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}.$$
2. Numerator, with $u = x^2/2$, $du = x\,dx$:
$$\int_0^\infty x\,\frac{e^{-x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,dx = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^\infty e^{-u}\,du = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.$$
3. Denominator: $P(X>0) = \frac12$ by symmetry.
4. So
$$E[X\mid X>0] = \frac{1/\sqrt{2\pi}}{1/2} = \sqrt{\frac2\pi}\approx 0.798.$$

**Book note.** The book stops at step 2 and gives $1/\sqrt{2\pi}\approx 0.399$. That is $E[X\,\mathbf 1_{X>0}]$, not the conditional mean; it is missing the division by $P(X>0)=\frac12$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Integrate $x\varphi(x)$ over $x>0$ and stop.

**Why it fails.** On $\{X>0\}$ the density $\varphi$ only has total mass $\frac12$; a conditional density must integrate to 1, so it is $2\varphi(x)$ there.

**The trick.** $x\,e^{-x^2/2} = -\frac{d}{dx}e^{-x^2/2}$, so the integral is immediate. Sanity check: the answer must lie well inside $(0,\infty)$; $0.8$ is about where the right half of the bell "balances".

**Going further.** $E|X| = \sqrt{2/\pi}$ too (same calculation), and in general $E[X\mid X>a] = \frac{\varphi(a)}{1-\Phi(a)}$ (the inverse Mills ratio).
