## Problem

Two doors: one to your job offer, one to the exit. Each has a guard; one guard always lies, the other always tells the truth, and you don't know which is which. You may ask **one** guard **one** yes/no question. What do you ask?

## Solution

Ask: *"Would the other guard say that the door behind you leads to the offer?"*

The question passes through both guards, so it contains exactly one liar:

| you ask | his door is the offer | other guard would say | he reports |
|---|---|---|---|
| truth-teller | yes | no (liar) | **no** |
| truth-teller | no | yes (liar) | **yes** |
| liar | yes | yes (truthful) | **no** (lies) |
| liar | no | no (truthful) | **yes** (lies) |

The reply is always the opposite of the truth: **no ⇒ go through his door, yes ⇒ go through the other one**.

Equally valid: *"If I asked you whether your door is the offer, would you say yes?"* A liar lying about his own lie gives the truth.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Is your door the offer?" The truth-teller and the liar answer differently, and you can't tell who's who.

**Why it fails.** One unknown negation scrambles the answer.

**The trick.** Force an **even or a known** number of negations. Through both guards you always get exactly one lie, so invert it.
