## Problem

Let $B_t$ be a standard Brownian motion. What is the probability that $B_1 > 0$ and $B_2 < 0$?

## Solution

1. $X = B_1$ and $Y = B_2 - B_1$ are independent $N(0,1)$. The event is
$$X > 0,\qquad X + Y < 0 \iff Y < -X.$$
2. The joint density $\frac1{2\pi}e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2}$ depends only on the radius, so a region's probability is its angle over $2\pi$.
3. $\{x>0,\ y<-x\}$ is the wedge between the directions $-90°$ (negative $y$-axis) and $-45°$ (the line $y = -x$): $45°$.
$$P = \frac{45}{360} = \frac18.$$
Equivalently: $P(X>0)\cdot P(Y<0)\cdot P(|Y|>|X|) = \frac12\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac12$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Write the double integral and grind.

**Why it fails.** It works, but polar coordinates are hiding in plain sight.

**The trick.** Use independent increments to get two i.i.d. standard normals, then rotational symmetry: probabilities of wedges are angles. (For general times $s<t$: $P = \frac14 - \frac{1}{2\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{s/t}$.)
