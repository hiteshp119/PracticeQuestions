## Problem

$f$ is differentiable. Describe algorithms to solve $f(x) = 0$ and compare them.

## Solution

Let $x^*$ be the root and $e_n = |x_n - x^*|$.

1. **Bisection.** Start with $f(a_0) < 0 < f(b_0)$; by continuity a root lies between. Let $m = \frac{a_n+b_n}{2}$:
$$\begin{aligned}f(m) < 0 &\Rightarrow a_{n+1} = m,\\ f(m)>0 &\Rightarrow b_{n+1} = m.\end{aligned}$$
Bracket width $= (b_0 - a_0)/2^n$: linear convergence, about 3.3 steps per decimal digit, **guaranteed**.
2. **Newton.** Linearize at $x_n$ (first-order Taylor) and solve the line:
$$\begin{aligned}&f(x)\approx f(x_n) + f'(x_n)(x - x_n) = 0\\ &\Rightarrow\; x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}.\end{aligned}$$
Near a simple root, $e_{n+1}\approx \Big|\frac{f''(x^*)}{2f'(x^*)}\Big|\,e_n^2$: quadratic. Needs $f'$; can diverge or cycle from a poor start or where $f'\approx0$.
3. **Secant.** Replace $f'(x_n)$ by the slope through the last two points:
$$x_{n+1} = x_n - f(x_n)\,\frac{x_n - x_{n-1}}{f(x_n) - f(x_{n-1})}.$$
Order $\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\approx1.618$; one new $f$-evaluation per step and no derivative. Not guaranteed either.

**In practice:** combine them (Brent's method): secant/inverse-quadratic steps while they stay inside a bisection bracket, bisection otherwise.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Use Newton everywhere because it is fastest.

**Why it fails.** Newton trusts the tangent line. Far from the root, or near a flat spot, the tangent can throw $x_{n+1}$ far away (try $f(x) = \arctan x$ from $x_0 = 2$).

**The trick.** Every method answers "where does my local model of $f$ cross zero?" Bisection's model is just a sign; Newton's is the tangent; secant's is a chord. Better models converge faster but need more assumptions; a bracket buys safety.
