## Problem

Ms. Parker has two children. You see her walking with one of them (equally likely to be either), and that child is a boy. What is the probability that both children are boys?

## Solution

1. Families $BB, BG, GB, GG$, each $\frac14$; she walks with a uniformly random child.
2. $P(\text{seen child is } B) = \frac14\cdot1 + \frac14\cdot\frac12 + \frac14\cdot\frac12 + 0 = \frac12$.
3. $P(BB \text{ and seen } B) = \frac14$.
4.
$$P(BB\mid\text{seen }B) = \frac{1/4}{1/2} = \frac12.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Same as card 4.13: $\frac13$."

**Why it fails.** Seeing a boy is more likely in a $BB$ family (certain) than in a mixed one ($\frac12$), so $BB$ gets twice the weight of each mixed family: $\frac{1}{1 + \frac12 + \frac12} = \frac12$.

**The trick.** The answer depends on **how** the information was produced, not just on what it says. Ask: what is the probability of observing exactly this, under each hypothesis?
