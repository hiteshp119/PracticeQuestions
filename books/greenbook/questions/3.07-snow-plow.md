## Problem

Snow starts falling at a constant rate some time before noon. At noon a plow starts clearing a road; it removes a constant volume of snow per minute. By 1 pm it has gone 2 miles, and by 2 pm, 3 miles in total. When did the snow start? (Give hours before noon.)

## Solution

1. Let $t$ = hours after noon, and suppose the snow began $T$ hours before noon. Depth (so cross-section) grows linearly: $A(t) = k(t+T)$.
2. Volume per hour is fixed: $v(t)\,A(t) = q$, so
$$v(t) = \frac{c}{t+T}, \qquad c = \frac qk.$$
3. Distance:
$$x(t) = \int_0^t \frac{c}{s+T}\,ds = c\ln\frac{t+T}{T}.$$
4. Data: $x(1) = 2$, $x(2) = 3$. Divide to drop $c$:
$$\frac{\ln\frac{2+T}{T}}{\ln\frac{1+T}{T}} = \frac32 \;\Rightarrow\; \Big(\frac{2+T}{T}\Big)^2 = \Big(\frac{1+T}{T}\Big)^3.$$
5. Multiply by $T^3$:
$$\begin{aligned}T(2+T)^2 &= (1+T)^3\\ T^3+4T^2+4T &= T^3+3T^2+3T+1\\ T^2+T-1 &= 0.\end{aligned}$$
6. Positive root:
$$T = \frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx 0.618\text{ h} \approx 37.1\text{ min}.$$
The snow started at about **11:23 am**.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "The plow slowed from 2 mph to 1 mph, so…" and try to fit a straight line to speed.

**Why it fails.** Speed is not linear in time; it is $\propto 1/(t+T)$, so straight-line guesses give the wrong $T$.

**The trick.** Model the physics in one line (volume rate = speed × cross-section), integrate to a log, then take a **ratio** so the unknown constants $q$ and $k$ cancel. Only $T$ survives.

**Going further.** The answer is $1/\varphi$, the golden ratio's reciprocal, because the data (2 then 3 miles) gives exactly $T^2 + T = 1$.
