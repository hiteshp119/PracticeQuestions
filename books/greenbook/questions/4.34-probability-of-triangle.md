## Problem

A stick of length 1 is cut at two points chosen independently and uniformly. What is the probability that the three pieces can form a triangle?

## Solution

1. Three lengths summing to 1 form a triangle iff each is $<\frac12$ (triangle inequality: $a < b + c = 1 - a$).
2. Cuts $x,y$; first suppose $x<y$. Pieces $x$, $y-x$, $1-y$:
$$x < \tfrac12,\qquad y > \tfrac12,\qquad y - x < \tfrac12.$$
3. In the triangle $\{0<x<y<1\}$ (area $\frac12$) this region is the triangle with vertices $(0,\frac12), (\frac12,\frac12), (\frac12, 1)$, area $\frac18$.
4. By symmetry $x>y$ adds another $\frac18$:
$$P = \tfrac18 + \tfrac18 = \tfrac14.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Fix the first cut and average the chance that the second cut works.

**Why it fails.** It works, but it needs a piecewise integral.

**The trick.** Probabilities of two uniforms are areas; draw the three linear constraints. Alternative view: the pieces are a uniform point in a triangle (barycentric coordinates); "all $<\frac12$" is the middle quarter between the midpoints.
