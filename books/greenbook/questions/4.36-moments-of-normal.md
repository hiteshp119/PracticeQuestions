## Problem

For $X\sim N(0,1)$, compute $E[X^n]$ for $n = 1,2,3,4$.

## Solution

1. **Odd $n$:** the density is even and $x^n$ is odd, so $E[X^n] = 0$.
2. **MGF:**
$$\begin{aligned}M(t) &= \int \frac{e^{tx - x^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}dx\\ &= e^{t^2/2}\int\frac{e^{-(x-t)^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}dx = e^{t^2/2}.\end{aligned}$$
3. Since $M(t) = \sum_n E[X^n]\frac{t^n}{n!}$, match with
$$e^{t^2/2} = 1 + \frac{t^2}{2} + \frac{t^4}{8} + \cdots$$
4. $E[X^2] = 2!\cdot\frac12 = 1$, $E[X^4] = 4!\cdot\frac18 = 3$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Integrate $x^4 e^{-x^2/2}$ by parts twice.

**Why it fails.** Fine for $n = 4$, tedious beyond that.

**The trick.** The MGF packs all moments into one function: read them off its Taylor series instead of differentiating repeatedly. Kurtosis $E[X^4] = 3$ is the benchmark for "fat tails".
