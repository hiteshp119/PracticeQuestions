## Problem

If there is no closed-form formula for an option, how would you estimate its delta and gamma?

## Solution

1. Compute prices $f(S - \delta S)$, $f(S)$, $f(S + \delta S)$ by your pricer (Monte Carlo, tree, PDE).
2. Taylor: $f(S\pm\delta S) = f \pm f'\delta S + \frac12f''(\delta S)^2 \pm \frac16 f'''(\delta S)^3 + \dots$, so
$$\Delta\approx\frac{f(S+\delta S) - f(S-\delta S)}{2\,\delta S}\quad(\text{error }O(\delta S^2)),$$
$$\Gamma\approx\frac{f(S+\delta S) - 2f(S) + f(S-\delta S)}{(\delta S)^2}.$$
3. **Monte Carlo noise.** Each price has error $\sim\frac{s}{\sqrt M}$; dividing by $\delta S$ (or $\delta S^2$) amplifies it. Use **common random numbers** (the same normal draws for all three prices): the errors are then strongly correlated and cancel in the differences.
4. **Pitfalls.** $\delta S$ too large ⇒ bias; too small ⇒ noise. For discontinuous payoffs (digitals, barriers) finite differences of Monte Carlo prices are very noisy; use pathwise derivatives, likelihood-ratio weights, or smooth the payoff.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Run three independent simulations and difference them.

**Why it fails.** The difference of two independent noisy estimates is dominated by noise when the true difference is small.

**The trick.** Correlate the noise on purpose: with identical random paths, the only thing that changes between the three prices is $S$.
