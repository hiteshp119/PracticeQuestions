## Problem

A shuffled 52-card deck is dealt to 4 players, 13 cards each. What is the probability that each player gets exactly one ace?

## Solution

1. Place the 4 aces one at a time among the 52 positions (13 per hand).
2. Ace 1: anywhere.
3. Ace 2: 51 places left, 39 are in other hands: $\frac{39}{51}$.
4. Ace 3: 50 left, 26 in the two empty hands: $\frac{26}{50}$.
5. Ace 4: 49 left, 13 in the last hand: $\frac{13}{49}$.
$$P = \frac{39\cdot26\cdot13}{51\cdot50\cdot49}\approx 0.1055.$$

**Counting check.**
$$P = \frac{4!\cdot\frac{48!}{(12!)^4}}{\frac{52!}{(13!)^4}} = \frac{4!\,48!\,13^4}{52!} = \frac{24\cdot 13^4}{52\cdot51\cdot50\cdot49}.$$
Same number.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Multinomial counting with factorials of 52.

**Why it fails.** It works but the cancellation is error-prone.

**The trick.** Only 4 cards matter. Follow just the aces: each one only needs to avoid the hands already holding an ace.
