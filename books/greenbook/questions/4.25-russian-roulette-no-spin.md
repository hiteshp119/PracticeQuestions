## Problem

A single bullet is loaded into a 6-chamber revolver and the cylinder is spun once. Two players take turns pulling the trigger (no further spinning) until the gun fires; whoever is shot loses. Do you go first or second? What is your probability of losing?

## Solution

1. After the spin the bullet sits in chamber $1,\dots,6$ with probability $\frac16$ each, and pulls go through chambers in order.
2. Player 1 fires chambers 1, 3, 5; player 2 fires 2, 4, 6.
3.
$$P(\text{1st loses}) = \tfrac36 = \tfrac12 = P(\text{2nd loses}).$$
It doesn't matter.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "The first player faces a $\frac16$ risk before the second player is exposed at all, so go second."

**Why it fails.** If player 1 survives, player 2 then faces worse odds ($\frac15$, then $\frac13$ …). The early risk and the later risk balance exactly.

**The trick.** Decide **all the randomness first**. One spin fixes everything; then just ask which chambers are "yours".
