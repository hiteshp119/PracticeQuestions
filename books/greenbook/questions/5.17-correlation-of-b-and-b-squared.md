## Problem

Let $B_t$ be a Brownian motion. What is the correlation between $B_t$ and $B_t^2$?

## Solution

1. $B_t\sim N(0,t)$, so $E[B_t] = 0$ and $E[B_t^3] = 0$ by symmetry.
2.
$$\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Cov}(B_t, B_t^2) &= E[B_t^3] - E[B_t]E[B_t^2]\\ &= 0 - 0 = 0.\end{aligned}$$
3. So the correlation is $0$ (for $t>0$; both variances are positive: $t$ and $2t^2$).

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "$B^2$ is a function of $B$, so they must be strongly correlated."

**Why it fails.** Correlation measures only **linear** dependence. Large $|B|$ gives large $B^2$ on both sides of zero, so the linear trend cancels.

**The trick.** Uncorrelated ≠ independent. Any symmetric $X$ and an even function $g(X)$ are uncorrelated.
