## Problem

The probability of seeing at least one car on a highway during a 20-minute interval is $\frac{609}{625}$. Cars arrive at a constant rate. What is the probability of seeing at least one car in a 5-minute interval?

## Solution

1. Let $p$ = P(at least one car in 5 min). No car in 20 min = no car in each of four independent 5-minute blocks:
$$(1-p)^4 = 1 - \frac{609}{625} = \frac{16}{625}.$$
2.
$$1 - p = \Big(\frac{16}{625}\Big)^{1/4} = \frac25 \;\Rightarrow\; p = \frac35.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Divide by 4: $\frac{609}{625}/4\approx 0.24$.

**Why it fails.** "At least one" probabilities don't add over intervals (they'd exceed 1 for long intervals).

**The trick.** Work with the complement: "no car" probabilities **multiply** over disjoint intervals. With a Poisson rate $\lambda$: $e^{-20\lambda} = \frac{16}{625}$, so $e^{-5\lambda} = \frac25$.
