## Problem

Compute $\sqrt{37}$ to three decimal places without a calculator.

## Solution

1. $f(x) = x^2 - 37$, $f'(x) = 2x$, start at $x_0 = 6$.
2. Newton step:
$$\begin{aligned}x_1 &= x_0 - \frac{f(x_0)}{f'(x_0)} = 6 - \frac{36-37}{12}\\ &= 6 + \frac1{12} \approx 6.0833.\end{aligned}$$
3. Check: $\big(6+\frac1{12}\big)^2 = 37 + \frac1{144}\approx 37.0069$, slightly high; a second step gives $\sqrt{37}\approx 6.0828$. To three decimals: $6.083$.

**Same thing, two other ways.**
- Taylor for $\sqrt{x}$ at $36$: $\sqrt{37}\approx \sqrt{36} + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{36}}(37-36) = 6 + \frac1{12}$.
- Algebra: $(6+y)^2 = 37 \Rightarrow 12y + y^2 = 1$; dropping $y^2$, $y \approx \frac1{12}$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Trial and error: $6.1^2 = 37.21$, $6.08^2 = 36.97$, …

**Why it fails.** It works, but each digit costs another multiplication and a guess.

**The trick.** Linearize at a point you know. One Newton/Taylor step from a perfect square $a^2$:
$$\sqrt{a^2 + d} \approx a + \frac{d}{2a},$$
with error about $\frac{d^2}{8a^3}$. Newton's error **squares** each step, so a second step would give about six correct decimals.
