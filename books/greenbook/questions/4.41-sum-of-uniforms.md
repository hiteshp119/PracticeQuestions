## Problem

$X_1,\dots,X_n$ are i.i.d. uniform on $[0,1]$. Find $P(X_1+\dots+X_n\le1)$.

## Solution

1. The probability is the volume of the simplex $\{x_i\ge0,\ \sum x_i\le1\}$. Small cases: $1,\ \frac12,\ \frac16$. Guess $\frac1{n!}$.
2. **Induction.** Suppose $P(S_n\le t) = \frac{t^n}{n!}$ for $0\le t\le1$ (scaling a simplex by $t$ scales volume by $t^n$). Condition on $X_{n+1} = x$:
$$\begin{aligned}P(S_{n+1}\le1) &= \int_0^1 P(S_n\le 1-x)\,dx\\ &= \int_0^1\frac{(1-x)^n}{n!}dx = \frac{1}{(n+1)!}.\end{aligned}$$
3. So $P(S_n\le1) = \frac1{n!}$.

**Symmetry proof.** The partial-sum map $y_k = x_1 + \dots + x_k$ is triangular with unit diagonal, so it preserves volume. It sends the simplex onto $\{0\le y_1\le\dots\le y_n\le1\}$, one of the $n!$ equally sized ordering regions of the unit cube.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Use the CLT: $S_n\approx N(n/2, n/12)$.

**Why it fails.** $S_n\le1$ is far in the left tail where the normal approximation is poor.

**The trick.** Probability = volume, and volumes of scaled shapes scale by $t^n$. The partial-sum map makes it vivid: $\{\sum x_i\le1\}$ has the same volume as $\{y_1\le y_2\le\dots\le y_n\}$, one ordering out of $n!$.

**Going further.** The expected number of uniforms needed for the sum to exceed 1 is $\sum_{n\ge0}P(S_n\le1) = \sum\frac1{n!} = e$.
