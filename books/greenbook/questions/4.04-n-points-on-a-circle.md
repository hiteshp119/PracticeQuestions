## Problem

$N$ points are placed independently and uniformly on a circle. What is the probability that all of them lie within some semicircle?

## Solution

1. For each point $i$, let $E_i$: the other $N-1$ points all lie in the half-circle that starts at point $i$ and goes clockwise. Each other point lands there with probability $\frac12$, independently:
$$P(E_i) = \frac{1}{2^{N-1}}.$$
2. **All points in some semicircle ⇔ some $E_i$ happens**: take the semicircle, rotate it clockwise until its start hits a point; that point is $i$.
3. **The $E_i$ are disjoint** (with probability 1): if $E_i$ holds, the clockwise gap from the last point back to $i$ is more than half the circle, and any other start $j$ would have to cross that gap.
4. So
$$P = \sum_{i=1}^N P(E_i) = \frac{N}{2^{N-1}}.$$
Checks: $N = 2 \Rightarrow 1$, $N=3 \Rightarrow \frac34$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Fix one point and ask whether the others are within $180°$ of it, then integrate over positions.

**Why it fails.** The semicircle need not start or be centred at a given point; the integrals get messy.

**The trick.** Name the semicircle by its **first point clockwise**. That turns "exists a semicircle" into a disjoint union of $N$ simple events.

**Going further.** For an arc that is a fraction $x \le \frac12$ of the circle, the same argument gives $N x^{N-1}$.
