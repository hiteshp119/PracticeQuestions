## Problem

Two players bet on repeated rolls of two dice. A wins as soon as a sum of 12 appears; B wins as soon as two consecutive sums of 7 appear. What is the probability that A wins?

## Solution

1. $P(12) = \frac1{36}$, $P(7) = \frac6{36}$, other $= \frac{29}{36}$.
2. States: $S$ (no relevant history), $7$ (last roll was 7), plus absorbing A-wins / B-wins. Let $a$ = P(A wins):
$$\begin{aligned}a_S &= \tfrac1{36} + \tfrac6{36}a_7 + \tfrac{29}{36}a_S,\\ a_7 &= \tfrac1{36} + \tfrac6{36}\cdot 0 + \tfrac{29}{36}a_S.\end{aligned}$$
3. From the first: $\frac{7}{36}a_S = \frac1{36} + \frac6{36}a_7 \Rightarrow 7a_S = 1 + 6a_7$. Substitute $a_7$:
$$\begin{aligned}7a_S &= 1 + \tfrac6{36} + \tfrac{6\cdot29}{36}a_S\\ 252a_S &= 42 + 174a_S\\ a_S &= \tfrac{42}{78} = \tfrac7{13}.\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "A 12 is 1-in-36 per roll; two 7s is $\frac1{36}$ per *pair* of rolls, so A is twice as likely."

**Why it fails.** B's chances per roll are not $\frac1{72}$: a 7 can start a pair on any roll, and pairs overlap.

**The trick.** Keep only the history that matters (was the last roll a 7?) as the state. Then it is two linear equations.
