## Problem

Two bullets are put in **random** chambers of a 6-chamber revolver and the cylinder is spun. Your opponent pulled the trigger and survived. Before your turn, you may spin the cylinder again or not. Which should you choose?

## Solution

1. **Spin:** fresh uniform chamber, 2 of 6 loaded: $P(\text{shot}) = \frac13$.
2. **Don't spin:** the bullets are uniformly placed among the 5 chambers other than the empty one just fired (random placement + the opponent's chamber was empty). The next chamber is one of those 5:
$$P(\text{shot}) = \tfrac25.$$
3. $\frac13 < \frac25$: **spin**.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Nothing changed, so it doesn't matter."

**Why it fails.** You learned one chamber was empty. Without spinning, the 2 bullets now hide among only 5 chambers.

**The trick.** Compare the information in "the opponent survived" with a fresh draw. With random placement, learning about one chamber says nothing special about its neighbour, so all that changes is the denominator. Contrast card 4.28, where placement is correlated.
