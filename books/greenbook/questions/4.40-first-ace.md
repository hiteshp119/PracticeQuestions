## Problem

A standard 52-card deck is shuffled and cards are turned over one at a time. What is the expected number of cards turned over up to and including the first ace?

## Solution

1. Position of the first ace $= 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{48} I_i$, where $I_i = 1$ if non-ace $i$ comes before every ace.
2. Card $i$ and the 4 aces are in random relative order; card $i$ is first among these 5 with probability $\frac15$:
$$E[I_i] = \tfrac15.$$
3. Linearity:
$$E = 1 + 48\cdot\tfrac15 = 10.6.$$
4. In general, with $k$ special cards among $N$: $1 + \frac{N-k}{k+1} = \frac{N+1}{k+1}$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** $E = \sum_k k\,P(\text{first ace at } k)$ with hypergeometric terms.

**Why it fails.** Correct, but a long sum.

**The trick.** Linearity of expectation with indicators needs no independence. And symmetry: 4 aces cut the deck into 5 gaps, each averaging $\frac{48}{5}$ cards, so the first ace sits at $\frac{48}{5} + 1$.
