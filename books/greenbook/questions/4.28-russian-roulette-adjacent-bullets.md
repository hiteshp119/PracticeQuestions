## Problem

Now two bullets are placed in **adjacent** chambers, and the cylinder is spun. Your opponent pulled and survived. Should you spin before your pull?

## Solution

1. In firing order, label the chambers $1,2,3,4$ (empty) and $5,6$ (loaded), cyclically.
2. The opponent survived, so he fired chamber 1, 2, 3 or 4, each with probability $\frac14$.
3. **Don't spin:** the next chamber is loaded only if he fired chamber 4:
$$P(\text{shot}) = \tfrac14.$$
4. **Spin:** $P(\text{shot}) = \frac26 = \frac13$.
5. $\frac14 < \frac13$: **don't spin**.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Same as card 4.27: spin."

**Why it fails.** Adjacency makes chambers correlated: empty chambers come in a run of 4, so after an empty chamber the next is empty 3 times out of 4.

**The trick.** Draw the cylinder and count which observed states lead to danger. The answer flips because the information "the last chamber was empty" is informative about its neighbour here.
