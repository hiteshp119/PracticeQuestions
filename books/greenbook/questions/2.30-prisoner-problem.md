## Problem

100 prisoners each get a red or blue hat at random. Each sees everyone else's hat but not his own. They're called one at a time in random order, and each loudly guesses his own hat colour; correct guesses go free, wrong ones are executed. They may agree a strategy beforehand but cannot communicate afterwards. How many can they guarantee to save?

## Solution

Code red $=1$, blue $=0$. The first prisoner called (call him $P_0$) sees the other 99 hats.

1. $P_0$ says "red" if he sees an **odd** number of red hats, "blue" if even. That broadcasts
$$p = \sum_{j\ne 0} h_j \bmod 2.$$
2. Prisoner $i$ sees all hats except his own and $P_0$'s, and has heard every earlier guess, which is correct from prisoner 2 onwards. Knowing all other $h_j$ ($j\neq 0, i$):
$$h_i = \Big(p - \sum_{j\ne 0,i} h_j\Big) \bmod 2.$$
3. Everyone except $P_0$ is saved: **99 guaranteed**, and $P_0$ survives with probability $1/2$.

99 is the best possible: the first speaker has no information about his own hat.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Each prisoner guesses the colour he sees most often: about 50% survive.

**Why it fails.** Each person's own hat is independent of what he sees, so individual guessing can't beat 50%.

**The trick.** The prisoners can't learn their own hat from what they **see**, but they can from what they **hear**. The first guess spends one life to broadcast a **parity bit**, and that bit plus 98 visible hats pins down each remaining hat.
