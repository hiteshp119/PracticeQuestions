## Problem

Four people must cross a bridge at night. The bridge holds at most 2 people, they have one torch, and anyone crossing needs it, so a pair walks at the slower person's speed. Times: A 10 min, B 5 min, C 2 min, D 1 min.

What is the minimum total time to get all four across?

## Solution

Two candidate plans (times in minutes):

| plan | trips | total |
|---|---|---|
| D ferries everyone | $(A{+}D)\,10,\ D\,1,\ (B{+}D)\,5,\ D\,1,\ (C{+}D)\,2$ | $19$ |
| slow pair together | $(C{+}D)\,2,\ D\,1,\ (A{+}B)\,10,\ C\,2,\ (C{+}D)\,2$ | $\mathbf{17}$ |

Why nothing beats 17:

1. Every plan has 3 trips over and 2 back.
2. If A and B cross on **different** trips, the trips over cost at least $10 + 5 + 1$ and the two returns at least $1 + 1$: total $\ge 18$.
3. So A and B must cross **together**, and not on the first trip (one of them would have to bring the torch back, costing $\ge 5$). The best such plan is the 17 above. A brute-force search over all plans (this site's answer check runs one) agrees: $17$ is the minimum.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Let the fastest person (D) escort everyone: $10+1+5+1+2 = 19$.

**Why it fails.** It pays for A and B *separately*. B's 5 minutes is wasted when it could happen during A's 10.

**The trick.** Send the two slowest together so their times overlap, and pre-position a fast person on the far side to bring the torch back. General rule for 4 people with times $a\le b\le c\le d$: total $= \min(2a+b+c+d,\ a+3b+d)$. Here $\min(2+2+5+10,\ 1+6+10) = \min(19,17)=17$.
