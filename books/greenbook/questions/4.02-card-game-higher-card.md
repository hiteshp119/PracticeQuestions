## Problem

A 52-card deck has 4 cards of each rank $2,\dots,10,J,Q,K,A$. You draw a card, then the dealer draws one of the remaining 51. You win if your rank is strictly higher; ties and lower ranks go to the house. What is your probability of winning?

## Solution

1. Three exclusive outcomes: you higher, tie, you lower. By symmetry $P(\text{higher}) = P(\text{lower})$.
2. Tie: whatever you draw, 3 of the 51 remaining cards match it:
$$P(\text{tie}) = \frac{3}{51} = \frac1{17}.$$
3. So
$$P(\text{win}) = \frac{1 - \frac1{17}}{2} = \frac{8}{17}\approx 0.4706.$$

**Direct check.** Your rank is the $k$-th lowest ($k=1,\dots,13$) with probability $\frac1{13}$ and beats $4(k-1)$ cards:
$$\frac{1}{13}\sum_{k=1}^{13}\frac{4(k-1)}{51} = \frac{4\cdot 78}{13\cdot 51} = \frac8{17}.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Condition on your card and sum 13 cases.

**Why it fails.** It works, but it is slow and error-prone under pressure.

**The trick.** Same as card 4.01: find the symmetric pair of events and compute only the leftover (the tie), which is easy.
