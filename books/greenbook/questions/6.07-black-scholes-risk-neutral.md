## Problem

Derive the Black–Scholes formula for a European call on a non-dividend stock using the risk-neutral measure.

## Solution

1. Under $\mathbb Q$: $dS = rS\,dt + \sigma S\,dW$. Itô on $\ln S$: $d\ln S = (r - \frac12\sigma^2)dt + \sigma\,dW$, so
$$S_T = S\exp\Big(\big(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\big)T + \sigma\sqrt T\,\varepsilon\Big)$$
with $\varepsilon\sim N(0,1)$.
2. $S_T > K \iff \varepsilon > \frac{\ln(K/S) - (r - \frac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T} = -d_2$. So
$$\mathbb Q(S_T > K) = N(d_2).$$
3. The other term:
$$\begin{aligned}E\big[S_T\mathbf 1_{\varepsilon>-d_2}\big] &= Se^{rT}\int_{-d_2}^\infty \frac{e^{-\frac12\sigma^2T + \sigma\sqrt T\varepsilon - \frac12\varepsilon^2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}d\varepsilon\\ &= Se^{rT}\int_{-d_2}^\infty\frac{e^{-\frac12(\varepsilon - \sigma\sqrt T)^2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}d\varepsilon.\end{aligned}$$
With $u = \varepsilon - \sigma\sqrt T$ the lower limit becomes $-d_2 - \sigma\sqrt T = -d_1$: the integral is $N(d_1)$.
4. Discount:
$$\begin{aligned}c &= e^{-rT}\Big(Se^{rT}N(d_1) - KN(d_2)\Big)\\ &= SN(d_1) - Ke^{-rT}N(d_2).\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Integrate $(S_T - K)$ against the lognormal density directly in $S_T$.

**Why it fails.** It works but the substitution is messy.

**The trick.** Work in the normal variable $\varepsilon$; the "$S_T$" term is a Gaussian integral with a shifted mean (completing the square = changing to the stock measure). Reading the formula: $Ke^{-rT}N(d_2)$ = PV of paying $K$ times the risk-neutral probability of exercise; $SN(d_1)$ = PV of receiving the stock when exercised.
