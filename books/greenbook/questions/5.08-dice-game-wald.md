## Problem

You roll a die and are paid its face value. On 4, 5 or 6 you roll again; on 1, 2 or 3 the game ends (after paying that roll). Use Wald's equality to find the expected payoff.

## Solution

1. Rolls $X_i$ i.i.d., $E[X] = 3.5$. Stop at the first roll in $\{1,2,3\}$: $N$ is a stopping time (deciding to stop after roll $n$ uses only rolls $1..n$).
2. Each roll stops with probability $\frac12$: $N\sim\text{Geometric}(\frac12)$, $E[N] = 2$.
3. Wald:
$$E[S_N] = E[N]\,E[X] = 2\times3.5 = 7.$$
Same as card 4.39.

**Why Wald holds.** $S_N = \sum_n X_n\mathbf 1_{N\ge n}$ and $\{N\ge n\}$ depends only on $X_1..X_{n-1}$, so it's independent of $X_n$: $E[S_N] = \sum_n E[X]P(N\ge n) = E[X]E[N]$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "The last roll is always low (avg 2), the others high (avg 5), so Wald can't apply."

**Why it fails.** Conditioning on *which* roll is last biases individual rolls, but Wald works on the whole sum: the biases exactly cancel. Check: $1\times 2 + (E[N]-1)\times5 = 2 + 5 = 7$. ✓

**The trick.** For "keep going until…" games with i.i.d. steps, multiply the expected number of steps by the expected step.
