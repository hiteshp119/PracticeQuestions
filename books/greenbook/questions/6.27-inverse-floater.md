## Problem

An inverse floater has face value \$100, matures in 5 years, and pays an annual coupon rate of $30\% - 3r$ (semiannually; $r$ is the floating rate set at the start of each period). The yield curve is flat at 7.5%. What are its price and duration?

## Solution

1. **Replicate.** Long 4 fixed-rate bonds (7.5% coupon, \$100 face) and short 3 floating-rate bonds (\$100 face):
   - coupons: $4\cdot7.5\% - 3r = 30\% - 3r$ ✓;
   - principal: $400 - 300 = 100$ ✓.
2. **Price.** At a flat 7.5% curve the par bond and the floater are both worth \$100:
$$P = 4(100) - 3(100) = 100.$$
3. **Dollar durations** ($DD = -dP/dy$, semiannual compounding):
   - floater = a zero worth \$103.75 in six months: $DD_{\text{float}} = \frac{0.5\times103.75}{(1.0375)^2}\approx48.19$;
   - 5-year 7.5% par bond: $DD_{\text{fixed}} = \frac1{1.0375}\sum_{k=1}^{10}\frac{k}{2}\cdot\frac{CF_k}{1.0375^k}\approx410.4$.
4.
$$\begin{aligned}DD_{\text{inv}} &= 4(410.4) - 3(48.19)\approx1498,\\ D_{\text{inv}} &= \frac{1498}{100}\approx14.98.\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Duration can't exceed maturity: at most 5."

**Why it fails.** That holds for bonds with fixed, positive cash flows. Here the coupons *fall* when rates rise, adding to the price drop: effectively 4× leverage on a fixed bond, minus a short floater with tiny duration.

**The trick.** Price exotic fixed-income by replication: price and dollar duration are linear in the positions, so you only need the building blocks.
