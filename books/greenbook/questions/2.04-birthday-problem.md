## Problem

Your boss's birthday is one of:

| month | days |
|---|---|
| Mar | 4, 5, 8 |
| Jun | 4, 7 |
| Sep | 1, 5 |
| Dec | 1, 2, 8 |

The boss tells **you** only the month and your colleague **C** only the day. Then:

1. You: "I don't know the birthday, and C doesn't know it either."
2. C: "I didn't know, but now I do."
3. You: "Now I know too."

Your assistant heard all this and wrote down the date. Which date?

## Solution

1. **"C doesn't know either."** Days 2 and 7 appear once, so C would know at once if told 2 or 7. You are *sure* C doesn't know, so your month contains neither: not June (7), not December (2). Remaining: Mar 4, Mar 5, Mar 8, Sep 1, Sep 5.
2. **C now knows.** C's day must be unique among those five. Day 5 appears twice, so drop Mar 5 and Sep 5. Remaining: Mar 4, Mar 8, Sep 1.
3. **You now know.** If your month were March you'd still have two options. So the month is September:
$$\text{birthday} = \text{Sep } 1.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Try to simulate what each person "thinks" in words. It gets tangled fast.

**Why it fails.** Words hide the information. Each statement is really a filter on the set of possible dates.

**The trick.** Keep a table of candidates and apply one filter per statement. Be careful with statement 1: it's about *your* knowledge of C, so it removes whole **months**, not single days.
