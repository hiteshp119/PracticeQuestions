## Problem

You toss a fair coin. What is the expected number of tosses until you get $n$ heads in a row?

## Solution

**Recursion.** Let $E_n$ = expected tosses for $n$ heads in a row. Reach $n$ heads ($E_n$), then toss once more: H (½) finishes; T (½) starts over:
$$E_{n+1} = E_n + 1 + \tfrac12E_{n+1}\;\Rightarrow\; E_{n+1} = 2E_n + 2.$$
With $E_1 = 2$: $E_n = 2^{n+1} - 2$ (2, 6, 14, 30, …).

**Martingale (any pattern).**
1. Before each toss a new gambler arrives with \$1 and bets it on the next toss matching the pattern's first symbol, then lets everything ride on the following symbols (fair odds: double or nothing).
2. Each bet is fair, so (total held) − (total staked) is a martingale; at the stopping time $\tau$, $E[\text{held}] = E[\tau]$.
3. When $n$ heads appear, the gamblers who joined in the last $n$ tosses hold $2^n, 2^{n-1},\dots,2$; everyone else is broke:
$$E[\tau] = 2^n + 2^{n-1} + \cdots + 2 = 2^{n+1} - 2.$$
4. For a general pattern, a gambler who joined $k$ tosses before the end still holds money iff the last $k$ tosses equal the pattern's first $k$. HHTTHH: $k = 6, 2, 1$: $64 + 4 + 2 = 70$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "$n$ heads has probability $2^{-n}$, so wait $2^n$ tosses."

**Why it fails.** Runs overlap: a failed run of $n$ heads wastes all the heads tossed so far.

**The trick.** The casino of fair gamblers turns a waiting time into a payoff at the stopping time, and self-overlaps of the pattern are exactly the extra gamblers still alive.
