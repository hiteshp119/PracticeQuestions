## Problem

Player 1 announces a triplet of coin outcomes (e.g. HTH). Player 2 then announces a different triplet. A fair coin is tossed until one of the two triplets appears; its owner wins. If both play optimally, would you rather be player 1 or player 2? As player 2, what is your winning probability?

## Solution

1. **Pairwise odds.** For any two triplets, P(B before A) comes from a Markov chain on "longest suffix matching a prefix of either pattern" (as in card 5.04), or from Conway's formula.
2. **Player 2's reply** to $A = a_1a_2a_3$: choose $B = \bar a_2\,a_1a_2$. B ends with $a_1a_2$, the start of A, so B usually completes just before A can.

| Player 1 | Best reply | P(player 2 wins) |
|---|---|---|
| HHH | THH | 7/8 |
| HHT | THH | 3/4 |
| HTH | HHT | 2/3 |
| HTT | HHT | 2/3 |
| THH | TTH | 2/3 |
| THT | TTH | 2/3 |
| TTH | HTT | 3/4 |
| TTT | HTT | 7/8 |

3. Player 1 can do no better than one of HTH, HTT, THH, THT, and even then player 2 wins with probability $\frac23$. **Go second.**

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Find the best triplet and pick it first."

**Why it fails.** There is no best triplet: "beats" is not transitive (like rock-paper-scissors). THH beats HHT, TTH beats THH, HTT beats TTH, HHT beats HTT.

**The trick.** A pattern wins a race by **ending with the other pattern's beginning**. Then whenever the opponent's pattern starts to form, yours is one step from done.
