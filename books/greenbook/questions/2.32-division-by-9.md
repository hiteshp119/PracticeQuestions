## Problem

Give a rule for deciding whether an integer is divisible by 9, and prove it.

## Solution

Write $a = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k 10^k$ with digits $a_k$.

1. $10 \equiv 1 \pmod 9 \;\Rightarrow\; 10^k \equiv 1 \pmod 9$ for all $k\ge0$.
2. Therefore
$$a = \sum_k a_k 10^k \equiv \sum_k a_k \pmod 9.$$
3. So $9 \mid a \iff 9 \mid \sum_k a_k$.

Equivalently, $a - \sum_k a_k = \sum_k a_k(10^k - 1)$, and each $10^k - 1 = 99\ldots9$ is a multiple of 9.

**Bonus (11).** $10 \equiv -1 \pmod{11}$, so $a \equiv \sum_k (-1)^k a_k$: alternate the signs of the digits.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Remember the rule; struggle to prove it.

**Why it fails.** The interviewer asks for the proof, and the rule alone doesn't explain why.

**The trick.** Reduce the **base** mod the divisor. Base 10 is $1$ mod 9 and $-1$ mod 11, and that explains both rules (and gives you rules for 3, 99, 101…).
