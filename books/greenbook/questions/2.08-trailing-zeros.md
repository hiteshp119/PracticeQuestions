## Problem

How many trailing zeros does $100!$ have?

## Solution

Each trailing zero is a factor $10 = 2 \cdot 5$. In $100!$ there are far more 2s than 5s, so

$$\#\text{zeros} = v_5(100!) = \left\lfloor \tfrac{100}{5}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \tfrac{100}{25}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \tfrac{100}{125}\right\rfloor = 20 + 4 + 0 = 24.$$

- $\lfloor 100/5 \rfloor = 20$ multiples of 5 each give one 5.
- $\lfloor 100/25 \rfloor = 4$ of them (25, 50, 75, 100) give a second 5.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Count multiples of 10 (there are 10), or multiples of 5 (there are 20).

**Why it fails.** Multiples of 10 miss pairs like $4 \times 25$. Multiples of 5 miss that $25 = 5^2$ carries two 5s.

**The trick.** Legendre's formula: $v_p(n!) = \sum_{k\ge1} \lfloor n/p^k \rfloor$. Each term counts the numbers carrying at least $k$ factors of $p$.
