## Problem

$\rho_{xy} = \rho_{xz} = 0.8$. Using the fact that a correlation matrix is positive semidefinite, find the range of $\rho = \rho_{yz}$.

## Solution

1. The matrix
$$P = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0.8 & 0.8\\ 0.8 & 1 & \rho\\ 0.8 & \rho & 1\end{pmatrix}$$
must be positive semidefinite (it is a covariance matrix: $w^\top P w = \operatorname{Var}(\sum w_i u_i)\ge0$).
2. Its determinant must be $\ge 0$. Expanding along the first row:
$$\begin{aligned}\det P &= (1-\rho^2) - 0.8(0.8 - 0.8\rho)\\ &\quad + 0.8(0.8\rho - 0.8)\\ &= -\rho^2 + 1.28\rho - 0.28.\end{aligned}$$
3. So
$$\begin{aligned}\rho^2 - 1.28\rho + 0.28 &\le 0\\ (\rho - 1)(\rho - 0.28) &\le 0\\ 0.28\le\rho&\le1.\end{aligned}$$
4. The $2\times2$ principal minors, $1 - 0.64$ and $1 - \rho^2$, are $\ge0$ on this range, so every $\rho\in[0.28, 1]$ really is attainable.

**Book note.** The book says a symmetric matrix is PSD iff its leading (upper-left) minors are $\ge 0$. That test is right for positive **definite** ($>0$), but for semidefinite you need **all** principal minors $\ge0$: $\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ has leading minors $0, 0$ but is not PSD. Step 4 checks the rest.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Pick values of $\rho$ and try to build random variables with those correlations.

**Why it fails.** Constructing examples proves what *is* possible, not what isn't.

**The trick.** Use a necessary condition on the whole matrix: no portfolio can have negative variance. With one unknown entry, $\det P\ge0$ is a quadratic in $\rho$, and its roots are the bounds. Same answer as the angle picture of card 3.20; the matrix method scales to more variables.
