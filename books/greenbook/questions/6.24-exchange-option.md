## Problem

$S_1$ and $S_2$ are non-dividend stocks following geometric Brownian motions with correlation $\rho$. How would you price an exchange option paying $\max(S_{2,T} - S_{1,T}, 0)$ at $T$?

## Solution

1. **Numeraire $S_1$.** Payoff $= S_{1,T}\max(f_T - 1, 0)$ with $f = S_2/S_1$.
2. **Dynamics of $f$** (Itô, $dS_i = \mu_iS_i\,dt + \sigma_iS_i\,dW_i$):
$$\begin{aligned}\frac{df}{f} = &\big(\mu_2 - \mu_1 + \sigma_1^2 - \rho\sigma_1\sigma_2\big)dt\\ &+ \sigma_2\,dW_2 - \sigma_1\,dW_1,\end{aligned}$$
a GBM with volatility $\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - 2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2}$.
3. Under the measure with numeraire $S_1$, prices divided by $S_1$ are martingales, so $f$ is driftless. The value in units of $S_1$ is a Black–Scholes call with spot $f_0 = S_2/S_1$, strike 1, rate 0, volatility $\sigma$:
$$\frac{V}{S_1} = \frac{S_2}{S_1}N(d_1) - N(d_2).$$
4. Multiply by $S_1$:
$$\begin{aligned}V &= S_2N(d_1) - S_1N(d_2),\\ d_{1,2} &= \frac{\ln(S_2/S_1)\pm\frac12\sigma^2T}{\sigma\sqrt T}.\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Integrate over the joint lognormal distribution of $(S_{1,T}, S_{2,T})$.

**Why it fails.** A 2-D integral where a 1-D one will do.

**The trick.** Choose the units cleverly. In "shares of $S_1$" the strike is just 1 and interest rates disappear (both legs earn $r$). Only the volatility of the *ratio* matters, which shrinks when the stocks are highly correlated.
