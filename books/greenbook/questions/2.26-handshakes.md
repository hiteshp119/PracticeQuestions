## Problem

At a party, you meet 25 team members. Each shakes your hand, and others shake hands randomly among themselves. Without knowing the total number of handshakes, can you be sure that at least two people shook hands with the same number of people?

## Solution

- People: $26$ (you plus 25).
- Each person shakes hands with between $1$ (at least you) and $25$ others: **25 possible values**.

$$26 \text{ people} > 25 \text{ values} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{two people share a count.}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Try to build a party where all 26 counts differ.

**Why it fails.** You'd need values $0,\dots,25$, but $0$ and $25$ can't coexist: someone who shook all 25 hands shook the hand of the person with 0.

**The trick.** This holds in **any** group, even without the "everyone shakes your hand" condition: the values $0$ and $n-1$ exclude each other, so $n$ people fit into $n-1$ values.
