## Problem

Players A and B take turns tossing a fair coin, A first. The game ends the first time a head is immediately followed by a tail (HT), and the player who tossed that tail wins. What is the probability that A wins?

## Solution

1. Let $p = P(A\text{ wins})$. Condition on A's first toss.
2. **A tosses T.** No H is pending, so the game restarts with B in A's role: $P(A\mid T) = 1-p$.
3. **A tosses H.** Let $h$ = probability that the player who just tossed H (with the opponent about to toss) eventually wins. The opponent tosses:
   - T (½): opponent wins;
   - H (½): roles swap: the opponent is now the one who just tossed H.
$$h = \tfrac12\cdot 0 + \tfrac12(1 - h) \;\Rightarrow\; h = \tfrac13.$$
4. So
$$p = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac13 + \tfrac12(1-p) \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac32 p = \tfrac23 \;\Rightarrow\; p = \tfrac49.$$
5. Sanity check: A can't win on toss 1, B can win on toss 2 ($\frac14$), so $p<\frac12$. ✓

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** List sequences ending in HT and sum by parity of their length.

**Why it fails.** It's an infinite sum over sequences like $T^aH^bT$; doable but slow.

**The trick.** States, not sequences. All that matters is (who tosses next, is an H pending). Write one equation per state, and use symmetry ("restart with roles swapped" ⇒ $1-p$) to cut the number of unknowns.
