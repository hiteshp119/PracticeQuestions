## Problem

A bowl holds 100 noodles. Blindfolded, you pick two free ends at random and tie them together, and repeat until there are no free ends. What is the expected number of loops formed?

## Solution

1. With $n$ strands (noodles or partial chains) there are $2n$ free ends. Pick any end; its partner is uniform among the other $2n-1$.
2. Exactly one of them is the other end of the **same** strand: a loop forms (and leaves), probability $\frac1{2n-1}$. Otherwise two strands merge. Either way $n\to n-1$.
3. So
$$E[L_n] = E[L_{n-1}] + \frac1{2n-1},\qquad E[L_1] = 1.$$
4.
$$E[L_{100}] = \sum_{k=1}^{100}\frac1{2k-1}\approx 3.284.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Track the full configuration of chains.

**Why it fails.** Chain lengths don't matter, but tracking them makes the state space explode.

**The trick.** The only thing that matters is the number of strands, and every step reduces it by exactly one. Write loops as a sum of indicators "step $k$ closes a loop" and use linearity of expectation. Grows like $\frac12\ln n$: loops are rare.
