## Problem

25 horses each run at a fixed, distinct speed. A race has at most 5 horses and tells you only their order. What is the minimum number of races to identify the 3 fastest horses?

## Solution

1. **Races 1–5:** heats of 5. Call the heats $A,\dots,E$ with finishing orders $A_1>A_2>\dots$.
2. **Race 6:** $A_1, B_1, C_1, D_1, E_1$. Relabel so that $A_1 > B_1 > C_1 > D_1 > E_1$.
   - $A_1$ is the fastest overall.
   - $D$ and $E$ are out: their best is at most 4th.
   - In heat $C$ only $C_1$ can make top 3 (behind $A_1$, $B_1$).
   - In heat $B$, $B_1$ and $B_2$ can.
   - In heat $A$, $A_2$ and $A_3$ can.
3. **Race 7:** $A_2, A_3, B_1, B_2, C_1$. The top 2 here are places 2 and 3 overall.

So $7$ races. Why not 6: every horse must race, which takes at least 5 races. The 6th race has to compare all 5 heat winners (any of them could be fastest), and then you still don't know who is 2nd and 3rd behind the winner.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Race heats, then race the top 3 of each heat (15 horses, 3 more races).

**Why it fails.** It ignores what the race of winners tells you about *whole heats*.

**The trick.** A horse is out as soon as 3 horses are known to be faster. Draw the $5\times5$ grid (heats × place), sorted both ways after race 6: only the cells with fewer than 3 known-faster horses survive. That's a triangle of 6 cells, one of which is $A_1$.
