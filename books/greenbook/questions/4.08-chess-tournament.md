## Problem

A knockout chess tournament has $2^n$ players with strengths $1 > 2 > \dots > 2^n$; the stronger player always wins. Pairings in each round are random. What is the probability that players 1 and 2 meet in the final?

## Solution

1. The bracket is two halves of $2^{n-1}$ players; each half sends its best player to the final.
2. Player 1 is in one half and reaches the final. Player 2 reaches the final iff they are the best in their half, i.e. iff they are **not** in player 1's half.
3. Player 2 is equally likely to occupy any of the other $2^n - 1$ spots, $2^{n-1}$ of which are in the other half:
$$P = \frac{2^{n-1}}{2^n - 1}.$$
4. Round-by-round check: they avoid each other in round $k$ with probability $\frac{2^{n-k+1} - 2}{2^{n-k+1}-1}$; the product telescopes to the same value.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Multiply "don't meet in round 1", "don't meet in round 2", … conditional probabilities.

**Why it fails.** It works but hides why the answer is just above $\frac12$.

**The trick.** Ask what the event *means* structurally: "meet in the final" = "different halves". One counting step.
