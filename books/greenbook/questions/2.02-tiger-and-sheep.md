## Problem

100 tigers and 1 sheep live on an island with plenty of grass. Tigers prefer sheep to grass, but:

- only one tiger can eat the sheep at a time, and that tiger then **turns into a sheep**;
- every tiger is perfectly rational and wants above all to survive.

Will the sheep be eaten?

## Solution

Let $E(n)$ = "with $n$ tigers, the sheep gets eaten". A tiger that eats moves the game to $n-1$ tigers, with itself as the sheep, so

$$E(n) = \neg E(n-1).$$

1. $E(1)$ is true: a lone tiger has nobody to fear.
2. $E(2) = \neg E(1)$ is false: whoever eats would be eaten.
3. $E(3)$ is true, $E(4)$ false, and so on:
$$E(n) \iff n \text{ is odd}.$$

$n = 100$ is even, so **the sheep is not eaten**.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Picture 100 tigers circling and try to predict what a "typical" tiger does. There's no way to settle it by looking at one tiger.

**Why it fails.** Each tiger's decision depends on what happens *after* it eats, which is the same puzzle with one fewer tiger.

**The trick.** Shrink to $n=1,2,3$, spot that each case flips the previous one, and write the recursion $E(n)=\neg E(n-1)$. Same backward-reasoning idea as the pirates.
