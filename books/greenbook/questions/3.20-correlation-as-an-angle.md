## Problem

Random variables $x$, $y$, $z$ have $\rho_{xy} = 0.8$ and $\rho_{xz} = 0.8$. What are the maximum and minimum possible values of $\rho_{yz}$?

## Solution

1. Centre and scale each variable; view it as a unit vector (inner product = covariance). Then
$$\rho_{uv} = \cos\angle(u,v).$$
2. $\cos\theta = 0.8$ with $\theta = \angle(x,y) = \angle(x,z)$; so $\sin\theta = 0.6$.
3. **Max.** $y = z$ is allowed (angle 0): $\rho_{yz} = 1$.
4. **Min.** The angle between $y$ and $z$ is largest, $2\theta$, when they lie in one plane on opposite sides of $x$:
$$\begin{aligned}\rho_{yz} &= \cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta\\ &= 0.64 - 0.36 = 0.28.\end{aligned}$$
5. In general, with $y$ and $z$ on the cone of half-angle $\theta$ around $x$ and azimuth gap $\varphi$:
$$\rho_{yz} = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta\cos\varphi \in [\,0.28,\ 1\,].$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Both are highly correlated with $x$, so they must be highly correlated with each other: at least $0.8$?" Or "anything in $[-1, 1]$ is possible."

**Why it fails.** Correlation is not transitive in a simple way, but it is not free either: three correlations must form a valid (positive semidefinite) matrix.

**The trick.** Turn correlations into angles. $0.8 \leftrightarrow 36.9°$, so $y$ and $z$ are each within $36.9°$ of $x$, hence within $73.7°$ of each other, and $\cos 73.7° = 0.28$.

**Going further.** For $\rho_{xy} = a$, $\rho_{xz} = b$:
$$\rho_{yz} \in \Big[\,ab \mp \sqrt{(1-a^2)(1-b^2)}\,\Big].$$
Card 3.23 reaches the same range through the correlation matrix.
