## Problem

Explain implied volatility and the volatility smile. What does the smile imply about the Black–Scholes model?

## Solution

1. **Implied volatility.** Vega $= SN'(d_1)\sqrt T > 0$, so the Black–Scholes price is strictly increasing in $\sigma$ and can be inverted: implied vol is the $\sigma$ that matches the market price. Options are quoted in it.
2. **Smile / skew.** Plot implied vol against strike for one maturity:
   - currencies: a symmetric **smile**, higher for ITM and OTM than ATM;
   - equities: a **skew**, higher vol at low strikes (crash protection is expensive).
3. **Implication.** Black–Scholes assumes a single constant $\sigma$, i.e. a flat line. A smile means the market's risk-neutral distribution is not lognormal: fatter tails (jumps), skew, and volatility that is itself random and correlated with the price.
4. Models that fit the smile: local volatility, stochastic volatility (Heston), jump diffusion.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Implied vol is a forecast of future volatility."

**Why it fails.** It is a price quoted in vol units; it also contains risk premia and tail pricing.

**The trick.** Read Black–Scholes as a quoting convention: a "wrong number in the wrong formula to get the right price". The shape of the smile is then the market's correction to the lognormal assumption.
