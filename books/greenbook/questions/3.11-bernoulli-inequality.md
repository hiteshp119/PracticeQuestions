## Problem

Prove that $(1+x)^n \ge 1 + nx$ for every real $x > -1$ and every integer $n\ge 2$.

## Solution

**Proof 1 (Taylor).** Let $f(x) = (1+x)^n$: $f(0)=1$, $f'(x) = n(1+x)^{n-1}$, $f''(x) = n(n-1)(1+x)^{n-2}$.
1. Taylor at $0$ with Lagrange remainder: for some $\xi$ between $0$ and $x$,
$$(1+x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}(1+\xi)^{n-2}x^2.$$
2. $\xi > -1$ (it lies between $0$ and $x>-1$), so $(1+\xi)^{n-2} > 0$; also $n(n-1)>0$ and $x^2\ge0$.
3. The last term is $\ge 0$, so $(1+x)^n \ge 1+nx$. ∎

**Proof 2 (induction on $n$).**
1. $n = 2$: $(1+x)^2 = 1 + 2x + x^2 \ge 1+2x$.
2. Step: if $(1+x)^k \ge 1+kx$, multiply by $1+x>0$ (the sign is kept):
$$\begin{aligned}(1+x)^{k+1} &\ge (1+kx)(1+x)\\ &= 1 + (k+1)x + kx^2\\ &\ge 1+(k+1)x. \qquad\blacksquare\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Expand $(1+x)^n$ with the binomial theorem and drop the higher terms.

**Why it fails.** For $-1<x<0$ the dropped terms alternate in sign, so "dropping them" is not obviously safe.

**The trick.** $1+nx$ is the **tangent line** of $(1+x)^n$ at $x=0$, and the curve is convex on $x>-1$. A convex curve lies above every tangent line. The Taylor remainder is the algebraic form of that picture. The condition $x>-1$ is exactly what makes $1+x>0$ in both proofs.
