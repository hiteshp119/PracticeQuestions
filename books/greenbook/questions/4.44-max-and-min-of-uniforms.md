## Problem

$X_1,\dots,X_n$ are i.i.d. uniform on $[0,1]$. Find the CDF, the density and the expected value of $Z = \max X_i$ and of $Y = \min X_i$.

## Solution

1. **Max.** $P(Z\le x) = P(\text{all } X_i\le x) = x^n$, so
$$\begin{aligned}f_Z(x) &= nx^{n-1},\\ E[Z] &= \int_0^1 x\cdot nx^{n-1}dx = \frac{n}{n+1}.\end{aligned}$$
2. **Min.** $P(Y\ge x) = (1-x)^n$, so
$$\begin{aligned}F_Y(x) &= 1-(1-x)^n,\quad f_Y(x) = n(1-x)^{n-1},\\ E[Y] &= \int_0^1 P(Y > x)\,dx = \frac1{n+1}.\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Compute $E[\max]$ from the joint density of all $n$ variables.

**Why it fails.** An $n$-dimensional integral for a one-line answer.

**The trick.** Events about the max/min are "all" events, which factor under independence. Symmetry gives the means instantly: $n$ uniform points cut $[0,1]$ into $n+1$ gaps of equal expected length $\frac1{n+1}$, so the min is at $\frac1{n+1}$ and the max at $\frac{n}{n+1}$. Also $Y \overset{d}{=} 1 - Z$.
