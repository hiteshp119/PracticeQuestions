## Problem

Let $B_t$ be a Brownian motion and $Z_t = \sqrt t\,B_t$. What are the mean and variance of $Z_t$? Is $Z_t$ a martingale?

## Solution

1. $B_t\sim N(0,t)$, so
$$E[Z_t] = 0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_t) = t\cdot t = t^2.$$
2. Itô with $f(t,B) = \sqrt t\,B$: $f_t = \frac{B}{2\sqrt t}$, $f_B = \sqrt t$, $f_{BB} = 0$:
$$dZ_t = \frac{B_t}{2\sqrt t}\,dt + \sqrt t\,dB_t.$$
3. The drift $\frac{B_t}{2\sqrt t}$ is nonzero whenever $B_t\ne0$ (probability 1), so $Z$ is **not** a martingale.
Direct check: for $s<t$, $E[Z_t\mid\mathcal F_s] = \sqrt t\,B_s \ne \sqrt s\,B_s = Z_s$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Mean 0 at all times, so it's a martingale."

**Why it fails.** A martingale's best forecast of the future is its current value. Here, given $B_s$, the forecast is $\sqrt{t}\,B_s$, which keeps growing away from $Z_s$.

**The trick.** Use Itô and look at the $dt$ term. Martingale ⇔ drift zero.
