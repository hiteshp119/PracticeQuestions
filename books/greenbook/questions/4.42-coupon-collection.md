## Problem

Each cereal box contains one of $N$ coupon types, all equally likely and independent.

(A) What is the expected number of boxes needed to collect all $N$ types?

(B) After $n$ boxes, what is the expected number of distinct types?

## Solution

**(A)**
1. Let $X_i$ = extra boxes to get the $i$-th new type once $i-1$ are held. Each box is new with probability $p_i = \frac{N-i+1}{N}$, so $X_i$ is geometric:
$$E[X_i] = \frac{N}{N-i+1}.$$
2.
$$\begin{aligned}E\Big[\sum_i X_i\Big] &= \sum_{i=1}^N\frac{N}{N-i+1}\\ &= N\sum_{k=1}^N\frac1k = NH_N.\end{aligned}$$
(E.g. $N = 6$: $14.7$ boxes.)

**(B)**
1. $Y = \sum_{j=1}^N I_j$, $I_j = 1$ if type $j$ appears in the $n$ boxes.
2. $P(I_j = 0) = \big(1 - \frac1N\big)^n$, so
$$E[Y] = N\Big(1 - \big(1 - \tfrac1N\big)^n\Big).$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "$N$ boxes for $N$ types" or "a bit more than $N$".

**Why it fails.** The last few types are slow: the final one alone takes $N$ boxes on average.

**The trick.** Break a long wait into stages with constant success probability (geometric waits) and add their means; count distinct items with indicators. Both are linearity of expectation. (Similar: 18 balls into 10 boxes leave $10\cdot0.9^{18}\approx1.5$ boxes empty on average.)
