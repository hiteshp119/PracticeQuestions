## Problem

The Red Sox and the Rockies play a best-of-seven World Series. You want the payoff of a \$100 double-or-nothing bet on the Red Sox **winning the series**, but you can only bet on individual games (even odds). How much should you bet on each game?

## Solution

1. State $(i,j)$ = games won by Red Sox / Rockies; $f(i,j)$ = your net winnings so far. Targets:
$$f(4,j) = +100,\qquad f(i,4) = -100.$$
2. Betting $y$ on the Red Sox at $(i,j)$:
$$\begin{aligned}f(i+1,j) &= f(i,j) + y,\quad f(i,j+1) = f(i,j) - y\\ \Rightarrow\; f(i,j) &= \frac{f(i+1,j) + f(i,j+1)}{2},\\ y &= \frac{f(i+1,j) - f(i,j+1)}{2}.\end{aligned}$$
3. Fill backwards. E.g. $f(3,3) = 0$, bet $100$; $f(3,2) = \frac{100 + 0}{2} = 50$, bet $50$; … $f(1,0) = 31.25$, $f(0,1) = -31.25$, so at $(0,0)$:
$$\begin{aligned}f(0,0) &= 0,\\ y(0,0) &= \frac{31.25 - (-31.25)}{2} = 31.25.\end{aligned}$$

| $f(i,j)$ | $j=0$ | $j=1$ | $j=2$ | $j=3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $i=0$ | 0 | −31.25 | −62.5 | −87.5 |
| $i=1$ | 31.25 | 0 | −37.5 | −75 |
| $i=2$ | 62.5 | 37.5 | 0 | −50 |
| $i=3$ | 87.5 | 75 | 50 | 0 |

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Bet a fixed amount (e.g. \$25) on every game.

**Why it fails.** Series can end after 4 to 7 games; a fixed bet leaves you with different totals depending on the path.

**The trick.** This is option replication on a binomial tree: the series bet is a "digital option" on the score path, the per-game bet is the delta, and you build it backwards from the payoff. With fair odds the starting value is 0, as it must be.
