## Problem

You have two ropes. Each takes exactly 1 hour to burn, but burns unevenly, so half the length need not take half the time. How do you measure 45 minutes?

## Solution

Fact: a rope with $x$ minutes left, lit at both ends, burns out in $x/2$ minutes (the two flames meet after burning $x$ minutes of rope between them).

1. $t=0$: light rope 1 at both ends, rope 2 at one end.
2. $t=30$: rope 1 is gone. Rope 2 has $60-30 = 30$ minutes left. Light its other end.
3. Rope 2 now burns out after $30/2 = 15$ more minutes:
$$30 + 15 = 45.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Cut or fold the rope at "three quarters".

**Why it fails.** Uneven density means length tells you nothing about time.

**The trick.** Measure with **events**, not lengths. "Both ends lit" halves any remaining time, and "rope 1 burns out" is a timer for 30 minutes.
