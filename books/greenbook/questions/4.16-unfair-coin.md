## Problem

You have 1000 coins: one has heads on both sides, the other 999 are fair. You pick a coin uniformly at random and toss it 10 times; all 10 are heads. What is the probability you picked the two-headed coin?

## Solution

1. $U$: unfair coin, $B$: ten heads. $P(U) = \frac1{1000}$, $P(B\mid U) = 1$, $P(B\mid U^c) = 2^{-10} = \frac1{1024}$.
2. Bayes:
$$\begin{aligned}P(U\mid B) &= \frac{\frac1{1000}}{\frac1{1000} + \frac{999}{1000}\cdot\frac{1}{1024}}\\ &= \frac{1024}{1024 + 999} = \frac{1024}{2023}\approx 0.506.\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Ten heads in a row is a 1-in-1000 event, so it must be the unfair coin."

**Why it fails.** The unfair coin itself is also a 1-in-1000 pick. Two rare things compete.

**The trick.** Use **odds**: prior odds $1 : 999$, likelihood ratio $1024 : 1$, posterior odds $1024 : 999$, just over even. Each head doubles the odds, so about $\log_2 999\approx 10$ heads are needed to reach 50-50.
