## Problem

A bull call spread is long a call with strike $K_1$ and short a call with strike $K_2 > K_1$ (same maturity). What are the bounds on its price?

## Solution

1. Payoff at $T$:

| $S_T$ | $\le K_1$ | $K_1$ to $K_2$ | $\ge K_2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| payoff | 0 | $S_T - K_1$ | $K_2 - K_1$ |

2. $0\le\text{payoff}\le K_2 - K_1$, so
$$0 \le c_1 - c_2 \le e^{-rT}(K_2 - K_1).$$
3. The payoff also lies below the line through the origin and $(K_2, K_2 - K_1)$: payoff $\le\frac{K_2 - K_1}{K_2}S_T$. That many shares dominate the spread:
$$c_1 - c_2\le\frac{K_2 - K_1}{K_2}\,S.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Price both calls with Black–Scholes.

**Why it fails.** The question wants model-free bounds.

**The trick.** Any payoff squeezed between two simple payoffs (zero, a bond, a multiple of the stock) has its price squeezed between theirs.
