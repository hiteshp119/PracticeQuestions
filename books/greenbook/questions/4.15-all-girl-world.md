## Problem

In a society every couple wants a girl. Each child is a girl with probability $\frac12$, independently. Every couple keeps having children until they have a girl, then stops. What happens to the fraction of girls in the population?

## Solution

1. Each family ends with exactly **one girl**.
2. Boys per family: $K$ = number of boys before the first girl, $P(K = k) = 2^{-(k+1)}$:
$$E[K] = \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{k}{2^{k+1}} = 1.$$
3. Expected children per family: $1 + 1 = 2$, of which 1 girl. Over many families the fraction of girls is
$$\frac{E[\text{girls}]}{E[\text{children}]} = \frac12.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Families stop at a girl, so girls pile up."

**Why it fails.** Every single birth is still a fair coin flip. A stopping rule decides *how many* flips happen, not their outcomes.

**The trick.** Look at births, not families: an infinite stream of independent fair flips is 50% girls whatever rule chops it into families. (This is the optional-stopping idea of card 5.07 in disguise: a stopping rule can't tilt a fair game.)
