## Problem

500 ants are placed independently and uniformly on a 1-foot string. Each walks left or right (equally likely) at 1 foot per minute. When two ants meet head-on, both reverse direction instantly. An ant at an end falls off. What is the expected time until all ants have fallen off?

## Solution

1. **Collisions don't matter.** When two ants meet and reverse, swap their names: the picture is the same as two ants walking straight through each other. So the *set* of positions moves as if every ant walked straight.
2. A straight-walking ant at $x$ falls off at time $x$ (going left) or $1-x$ (going right). With $x$ uniform, either way the fall time is $U(0,1)$, independent across ants.
3. All ants are gone when the last ghost falls off: the max of 500 i.i.d. uniforms. By card 4.44:
$$E[T] = \frac{500}{501}\approx 0.998 \text{ min}.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Simulate the collisions and track each ant.

**Why it fails.** Collisions chain, so tracking individual ants is a mess.

**The trick.** Ants are indistinguishable; only the set of positions matters for "when are they all gone". Relabel at each collision and the interaction disappears.
