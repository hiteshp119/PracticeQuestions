## Problem

What is a straddle, and when would you want to buy one?

## Solution

1. **Long straddle** = long call + long put, same strike $K$ and maturity $T$:
$$\text{payoff} = (S_T - K)^+ + (K - S_T)^+ = |S_T - K|.$$
2. You profit if $|S_T - K|$ exceeds the premium: a **large move in either direction**.
3. **Volatility bet.** At the money, each leg costs $\approx0.4\sigma_{\text{imp}}S\sqrt T$ (card 6.14), so the straddle is almost linear in implied vol. Buy if you expect realized volatility above implied.
4. **Caveat.** An ATM straddle starts with delta $\approx0$, but as $S$ moves delta becomes nonzero, so it carries price risk too. A pure volatility bet uses a variance swap, paying $N(\sigma^2_{\text{realized}} - K_{\text{var}})$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "It's for when you don't know the direction."

**Why it fails.** If you just don't know the direction but expect a normal-size move, the premium already prices that in.

**The trick.** You're buying volatility: you win only if the move is bigger than the market expects.
