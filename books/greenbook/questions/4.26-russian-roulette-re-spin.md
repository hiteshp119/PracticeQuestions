## Problem

One bullet in a 6-chamber revolver, but now the cylinder is spun again before **every** pull. Players alternate until someone is shot. Should you go first or second, and what is your probability of losing?

## Solution

1. Let $p$ = probability the first player loses. Each pull fires with probability $\frac16$, independently.
2. First pull: fires ($\frac16$) ⇒ first player loses. Otherwise ($\frac56$) the other player is now "first":
$$p = \tfrac16 + \tfrac56(1 - p) \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac{11}{6}p = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; p = \tfrac6{11}.$$
3. Go second: you lose with probability $1 - \frac6{11} = \frac5{11}\approx 0.455$.

Check with a series: $p = \frac16\sum_{k\ge0}\big(\frac{25}{36}\big)^k = \frac{1/6}{11/36} = \frac6{11}$. ✓

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Same as without re-spinning: $\frac12$."

**Why it fails.** Re-spinning makes every pull a fresh $\frac16$ chance, so whoever pulls first is always exposed first and the advantage never evens out.

**The trick.** "Survive one round ⇒ roles swap" gives a one-line equation. Compare card 4.25: same story, but there the bullet's position is fixed, and the later pulls get riskier.
