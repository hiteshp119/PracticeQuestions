## Problem

You don't know the stochastic process for the stock, but you observe European call prices $c(K)$ for all strikes $K$ with maturity $T$. Can you find the risk-neutral density of $S_T$?

## Solution

1. Risk-neutral pricing:
$$c(K) = e^{-rT}\int_K^\infty(s-K)f(s)\,ds.$$
2. Leibniz rule (the boundary term $(K - K)f(K) = 0$ vanishes):
$$\frac{\partial c}{\partial K} = -e^{-rT}\int_K^\infty f(s)\,ds = -e^{-rT}\,\mathbb Q(S_T > K).$$
3. Again:
$$\frac{\partial^2c}{\partial K^2} = e^{-rT}f(K)\;\Rightarrow\; f(K) = e^{rT}\frac{\partial^2c}{\partial K^2}.$$
4. **In practice:** with strikes spaced $\Delta K$, the butterfly $c(K-\Delta K) - 2c(K) + c(K+\Delta K)$ pays a small triangle centred at $K$ with area $(\Delta K)^2$, so
$$f(K)\approx e^{rT}\frac{c(K-\Delta K) - 2c(K) + c(K+\Delta K)}{(\Delta K)^2}.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Fit a model (e.g. Black–Scholes with some $\sigma$) and read off its lognormal density.

**Why it fails.** It imposes the model's shape and throws away what the smile is telling you.

**The trick.** Calls of all strikes span every payoff; a tight butterfly is (almost) an Arrow–Debreu security paying 1 if $S_T\approx K$. Its price is the discounted probability density. Convexity of $c$ in $K$ is exactly $f\ge0$ (compare card 6.04).
