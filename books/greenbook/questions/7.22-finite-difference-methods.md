## Problem

Explain the explicit, implicit, and Crank–Nicolson finite difference methods for solving the heat equation $u_\tau = u_{xx}$ (to which Black–Scholes reduces, card 6.08).

## Solution

Grid: $\tau_n = n\Delta\tau$, $x_j = x_0 + j\Delta x$, $u^n_j\approx u(x_j,\tau_n)$; $\alpha = \frac{\Delta\tau}{\Delta x^2}$; boundary values given.

1. **Explicit.** Forward difference in time, central in space at level $n$:
$$\begin{aligned}\frac{u^{n+1}_j - u^n_j}{\Delta\tau} &= \frac{u^n_{j+1} - 2u^n_j + u^n_{j-1}}{\Delta x^2}\\ \Rightarrow\; u^{n+1}_j &= \alpha u^n_{j-1} + (1-2\alpha)u^n_j + \alpha u^n_{j+1}.\end{aligned}$$
Each new value is computed directly. Stable only if $\alpha\le\frac12$.
2. **Implicit.** Central space difference at the **new** level $n+1$:
$$-\alpha u^{n+1}_{j-1} + (1+2\alpha)u^{n+1}_j - \alpha u^{n+1}_{j+1} = u^n_j.$$
A tridiagonal system per step ($O(J)$ by the Thomas algorithm); unconditionally stable; error $O(\Delta\tau + \Delta x^2)$.
3. **Crank–Nicolson.** Average the space differences at $n$ and $n+1$ (centred at $n+\frac12$):
$$\begin{aligned}\frac{u^{n+1}_j - u^n_j}{\Delta\tau} &= \frac12\Big(\delta_x^2u^n_j + \delta_x^2u^{n+1}_j\Big),\\ \delta_x^2u_j &= \frac{u_{j+1} - 2u_j + u_{j-1}}{\Delta x^2}.\end{aligned}$$
Also tridiagonal and unconditionally stable, with second-order accuracy $O(\Delta\tau^2 + \Delta x^2)$. (Can oscillate with non-smooth payoffs; a few implicit steps first fix that.)

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Always use explicit: it's the simplest.

**Why it fails.** It forces $\Delta\tau\le\frac12\Delta x^2$: refining space by 10× needs 100× more time steps.

**The trick.** Where you evaluate the spatial derivative decides stability and accuracy. Paying for a tridiagonal solve (cheap) buys freedom to take large time steps.
