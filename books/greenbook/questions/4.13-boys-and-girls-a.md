## Problem

A company holds a dinner for working mothers with at least one son. Ms. Jackson, who has two children, is invited. What is the probability that both of her children are boys?

## Solution

1. Equally likely families (older, younger): $BB, BG, GB, GG$, each $\frac14$.
2. Invitation ⇔ at least one boy: $\{BB, BG, GB\}$, probability $\frac34$.
3.
$$P(BB\mid\ge1\ B) = \frac{P(BB)}{P(\ge1\ B)} = \frac{1/4}{3/4} = \frac13.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "One child is a boy; the other is a boy or girl with equal chance: $\frac12$."

**Why it fails.** "At least one boy" doesn't say *which* child is the boy. $BG$ and $GB$ are two different families, both consistent with the information.

**The trick.** Write the sample space and cross out what the information rules out. Compare card 4.14, where the information is about a *specific* child.
