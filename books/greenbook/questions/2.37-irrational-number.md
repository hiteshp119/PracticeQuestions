## Problem

Prove that $\sqrt 2$ is irrational.

## Solution

Suppose $\sqrt2 = m/n$ with integers $m, n$ having no common factor.

1. $m^2 = 2n^2$, so $m^2$ is even, hence $m$ is even: $m = 2k$.
2. $4k^2 = 2n^2 \Rightarrow n^2 = 2k^2$, so $n$ is even.
3. Both even contradicts "no common factor". Hence $\sqrt 2 \notin \mathbb Q$. $\blacksquare$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Compute many digits of $\sqrt2$ and look for a repeat.

**Why it fails.** No finite check proves a number never repeats.

**The trick.** **Contradiction + parity (or unique factorisation):** in $m^2 = 2n^2$ the prime 2 appears an even number of times on the left and an odd number of times on the right. The same argument shows $\sqrt{k}$ is irrational unless $k$ is a perfect square.
