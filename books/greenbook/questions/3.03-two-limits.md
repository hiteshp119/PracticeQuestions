## Problem

Find

(a) $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{e^x}{x^2}$ and (b) $\displaystyle \lim_{x\to 0^+} x^2 \ln x$.

## Solution

**(a)** Form $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. L'Hospital twice:
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{e^x}{x^2} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{e^x}{2x} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{e^x}{2} = \infty.$$

**(b)** Form $0\cdot(-\infty)$. Rewrite as a quotient, form $\frac{-\infty}{\infty}$:
$$\begin{aligned}\lim_{x\to0^+} x^2\ln x &= \lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{\ln x}{x^{-2}} = \lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{1/x}{-2x^{-3}}\\ &= \lim_{x\to0^+}\Big(-\frac{x^2}{2}\Big) = 0.\end{aligned}$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** In (b), "$0$ times something huge" could be anything; plugging in gives no answer.

**Why it fails.** $0\cdot\infty$ is not a form L'Hospital accepts directly.

**The trick.** Move one factor to the denominator so it becomes $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. Choose the factor whose reciprocal is easy to differentiate ($x^{-2}$, not $1/\ln x$).

**Going further.** The growth ladder: for any $r>0$,
$$\ln x \ll x^r \ll e^x \quad (x\to\infty),$$
so $e^x/x^n \to\infty$ for every $n$, and $x^r\ln x \to 0$ as $x\to0^+$.
