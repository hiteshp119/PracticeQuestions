## Problem

Four switches outside a room; exactly one controls the bulb inside. All are off. You can flip switches as often as you like, but can't see the bulb from outside. How many times must you enter the room to know which switch controls it?

## Solution

1. Turn on switches 1 and 2. Wait several minutes.
2. Turn off switch 2, turn on switch 3. Enter immediately.

| bulb | switch |
|---|---|
| on, warm | 1 |
| off, warm | 2 |
| on, cold | 3 |
| off, cold | 4 |

So **one** trip.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** On/off tells you 1 bit per visit, so you need $\lceil \log_2 4 \rceil = 2$ visits.

**Why it fails.** It assumes the bulb gives one bit of information per visit.

**The trick.** Count *all* the signals. The bulb's temperature is a second bit. Interviewers like this as an "information-counting" check: how many outcomes can one observation distinguish?
