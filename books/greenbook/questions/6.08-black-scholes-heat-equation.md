## Problem

How can you derive the Black–Scholes call price by solving the Black–Scholes–Merton PDE directly?

## Solution

1. Start: $V_t + rSV_S + \frac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS} = rV$, $V(S,T) = (S-K)^+$.
2. **Log price, time to maturity:** $y = \ln S$, $\tau = T-t$. Then $SV_S = V_y$, $S^2V_{SS} = V_{yy} - V_y$:
$$-V_\tau + \big(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\big)V_y + \tfrac12\sigma^2V_{yy} = rV.$$
3. **Discount:** $u = e^{r\tau}V$ removes the $rV$ term:
$$u_\tau = \big(r - \tfrac12\sigma^2\big)u_y + \tfrac12\sigma^2u_{yy}.$$
4. **Moving frame:** $x = y + (r - \frac12\sigma^2)\tau$ removes the drift:
$$u_\tau = \tfrac12\sigma^2u_{xx},\qquad u(x,0) = (e^x - K)^+.$$
5. Heat equation solution:
$$u(x,\tau) = \int\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2\tau}}e^{-\frac{(x-\xi)^2}{2\sigma^2\tau}}(e^\xi - K)^+\,d\xi,$$
which is $E[(S_T - K)^+]$ with $\ln S_T\sim N(\ln S + (r-\frac12\sigma^2)\tau,\ \sigma^2\tau)$. Evaluating as in card 6.07 and multiplying by $e^{-r\tau}$:
$$V = SN(d_1) - Ke^{-r\tau}N(d_2).$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Guess a solution form and plug in.

**Why it fails.** There's no obvious guess; the variable coefficients $S$, $S^2$ hide the structure.

**The trick.** Each substitution removes one nuisance: logs fix the $S$, $S^2$ coefficients, discounting removes $rV$, a moving frame removes the drift. What's left is pure diffusion, whose solution is "average the payoff against a Gaussian", i.e. risk-neutral pricing (Feynman–Kac).
