## Problem

What is the difference between futures and forwards? If the underlying asset's price is strongly positively correlated with interest rates, and rates are stochastic, which has the higher price: futures or forwards? Why?

## Solution

1. **Differences.**
   - Futures: exchange-traded, standardized, cleared, **marked to market daily** with margin.
   - Forwards: over-the-counter, customizable, single settlement at maturity (counterparty risk).
2. **Deterministic rates:** the two prices are equal: $F = Se^{(r + u - y)T}$ ($u$: storage cost, $y$: dividend/convenience yield or foreign rate).
3. **Stochastic, positively correlated rates.** The long futures holder receives gains daily. Gains tend to arrive when the asset is up, i.e. when rates are high: reinvested at high rates. Losses arrive when rates are low: financed cheaply. Marking to market is therefore worth something to the long, so the futures price must be **higher** than the forward price to compensate. (Negative correlation reverses it.)

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Same asset, same delivery date, same price."

**Why it fails.** The *timing* of cash flows differs, and timing matters when rates are random and correlated with those cash flows.

**The trick.** Ask when the cash arrives and what it can earn then. Correlation between P&L and reinvestment rate is what separates the two prices (a convexity adjustment).
