## Problem

A poker hand is 5 cards from a 52-card deck (13 ranks × 4 suits). Find the probability of (a) four of a kind, (b) a full house (three of one rank, two of another), (c) two pairs.

## Solution

Total: $\binom{52}{5} = 2{,}598{,}960$.

1. **Four of a kind.** Rank of the four: $13$. Fifth card: any of the other $48$.
$$13\cdot 48 = 624,\qquad P \approx 2.4\times10^{-4}.$$
2. **Full house.** Triple rank $13$, its suits $\binom43$; pair rank $12$ (different, and the roles differ so order matters), its suits $\binom42$:
$$13\cdot4\cdot12\cdot6 = 3{,}744,\qquad P\approx 1.44\times10^{-3}.$$
3. **Two pairs.** Pair ranks $\binom{13}2 = 78$ (same role, unordered), suits $\binom42^2 = 36$, fifth card avoids both ranks: $52 - 8 = 44$:
$$78\cdot36\cdot44 = 123{,}552,\qquad P\approx 0.0475.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** For two pairs: $13\cdot\binom42\cdot12\cdot\binom42\cdot44$.

**Why it fails.** It counts every hand twice (which pair you picked "first"), giving double the answer.

**The trick.** Ask whether the groups you choose are interchangeable. Pairs in "two pairs" are: use $\binom{13}{2}$. The triple and pair in a full house are not: use $13\cdot12$.
