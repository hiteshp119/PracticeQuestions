## Problem

A theater gives a free ticket to the first person in line whose birthday is the same as someone ahead of them. You may choose your position. With 365 equally likely birthdays, which position maximizes your chance of winning?

## Solution

1. In position $n$ you win iff the first $n-1$ birthdays are all different **and** yours equals one of them:
$$P(n) = \underbrace{\prod_{k=0}^{n-2}\frac{365-k}{365}}_{\text{no earlier match}}\cdot\frac{n-1}{365}.$$
2. Ratio of consecutive positions:
$$\frac{P(n+1)}{P(n)} = \frac{365-(n-1)}{365}\cdot\frac{n}{n-1}.$$
3. $P(n+1) > P(n)$ iff $n(366-n) > 365(n-1)$, i.e.
$$n^2 - n < 365 \iff n \le 19.$$
4. So $P$ increases up to $n = 20$ and decreases after: choose **position 20** ($P(20)\approx 0.0323$).

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Stand far back so many birthdays are already "taken".

**Why it fails.** Far back, someone ahead of you has almost surely already won.

**The trick.** A trade-off between "a match is possible for me" (grows like $n$) and "no one before me matched" (shrinks like $e^{-n^2/730}$). Maximize the product by comparing neighbours: the optimum is near $\sqrt{365}\approx 19$.
