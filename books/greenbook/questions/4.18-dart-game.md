## Problem

Jason throws darts at the centre of a board with constant skill (throws are i.i.d.). His second dart lands farther from the centre than his first. He throws a third. What is the probability it is also farther from the centre than the first?

## Solution

1. Rank the 3 distances; all $6$ orders are equally likely.
2. Condition on $d_1 < d_2$ (3 orders remain). Dart 3 beats dart 1 only if it is the best of all three: $d_3 < d_1 < d_2$, one order out of three.
3.
$$P(d_3 > d_1 \mid d_1 < d_2) = 1 - \tfrac13 = \tfrac23.$$

**General version.** If darts $2,\dots,n$ are all worse than dart 1, dart $n+1$ is worse than dart 1 unless it is the best of all $n+1$:
$$P = 1 - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{n}{n+1}.$$
"Throw $n+1$ is the best" is independent of the order among the first $n$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Each throw is independent, so $\frac12$."

**Why it fails.** The *throws* are independent, but you've learned that dart 1 was good relative to dart 2; dart 1 is now likely better than a typical throw.

**The trick.** Think in **ranks**. With i.i.d. continuous throws every ordering is equally likely, so conditional probabilities become counting orders.
