## Problem

Show that among any 6 people at a party, either at least 3 have all met each other before, or at least 3 are mutual strangers.

## Solution

1. Pick any person $P$. They have $5$ relationships, each "met" or "not met". By pigeonhole, at least $\lceil 5/2 \rceil = 3$ are the same type.
2. **Case A:** $P$ has met $X, Y, Z$.
   - If any two of $X,Y,Z$ have met, they and $P$ form 3 mutual acquaintances.
   - If none have, $X, Y, Z$ are 3 mutual strangers.
3. **Case B:** $P$ has met none of $X, Y, Z$. Same argument with the roles swapped.

Either way the claim holds. (This is the Ramsey number $R(3,3) = 6$; with 5 people it can fail: arrange them in a pentagon where neighbours know each other.)

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Enumerate all acquaintance patterns: $2^{15}$ of them.

**Why it fails.** Far too many cases to check by hand.

**The trick.** Pigeonhole on **one person's** relationships, then a two-way split. Think of a complete graph with edges coloured in two colours: the claim says a one-colour triangle is unavoidable.
