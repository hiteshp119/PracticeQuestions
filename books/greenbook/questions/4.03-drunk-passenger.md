## Problem

100 passengers board in order; passenger $k$ holds a ticket for seat $k$. Passenger 1 is drunk and sits in a uniformly random seat. Each later passenger takes their own seat if it is free, otherwise a uniformly random free seat. You are passenger 100. What is the probability you sit in seat 100?

## Solution

1. The last free seat is always **seat 1 or seat 100**: any other seat $k$ is taken by passenger $k$ at the latest when they board.
2. The process is decided by the first random chooser to pick seat 1 or seat 100:
   - seat 1 first ⇒ everyone after sits correctly, you get seat 100;
   - seat 100 first ⇒ you don't.
3. Every random choice is uniform over free seats, and seats 1 and 100 are both free until one of them is taken, so at every step they are equally likely. Hence
$$P(\text{you get seat }100) = \tfrac12.$$
4. Check with $n = 2$: passenger 1 picks seat 1 or 2 with probability $\frac12$ each. ✓

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Condition on passenger 1's seat and recurse through all 99 cases.

**Why it fails.** The recursion works (it gives $\frac12$ for every $n\ge2$) but is long.

**The trick.** Ask "which seat is left at the end?" Only seats 1 and 100 are candidates, and nothing in the process favours one over the other.
