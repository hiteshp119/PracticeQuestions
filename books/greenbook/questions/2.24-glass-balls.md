## Problem

You have two identical glass balls and a 100-storey building. A ball breaks if dropped from floor $X$ or higher and survives below $X$. Find $X$ with the fewest drops in the **worst case**. (This problem appears in the book under the "Counterfeit coins I" heading.)

## Solution

Fix a worst-case budget $N$.

1. Drop ball 1 from floor $N$. If it breaks, test floors $1,\dots,N-1$ one by one with ball 2: at most $1 + (N-1) = N$ drops.
2. If not, one drop is used, so the next jump must be $N-1$ floors (to floor $2N-1$), then $N-2$, …
3. Floors covered with $N$ drops:
$$N + (N-1) + \dots + 1 = \frac{N(N+1)}{2} \ge 100 \;\Rightarrow\; N = 14 \quad (\tfrac{14\cdot15}{2} = 105).$$

Ball 1 goes to floors $14, 27, 39, 50, 60, 69, 77, 84, 90, 95, 99, 100$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Binary search: drop at 50. If it breaks you're left with one ball and 49 floors, so up to 50 drops.

**Why it fails.** Binary search assumes unlimited balls. With one ball left you must go up one floor at a time.

**The trick.** **Balance the worst case**: every branch should cost the same total. Each extra drop of ball 1 must leave ball 2 one floor less to check, so the steps shrink by one: $N, N-1, \dots$. With $k$ balls the same reasoning uses binomial sums.
