## Problem

51 ants sit anywhere on a square of side 1. You have a round glass of radius $1/7$. Can you always place it to cover at least 3 ants?

## Solution

1. Cut the square into $5\times5 = 25$ cells of side $1/5$.
2. Generalised pigeonhole: $51 = 2\cdot 25 + 1$ ants ⇒ some cell has $\ge 3$ ants.
3. A cell's circumscribed circle has radius = half its diagonal:
$$r = \frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac15 = \frac{\sqrt2}{10} \approx 0.1414 \;\le\; \frac17 \approx 0.1429.$$
So a glass centred on that cell covers it, and covers $\ge 3$ ants.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Think about the densest cluster, or about the ants' positions.

**Why it fails.** The positions are adversarial; you need a guarantee that holds for every arrangement.

**The trick.** Choose the number of regions from the numbers given: $51 = 2\cdot25+1$ suggests 25 regions. Then check the geometry fits: the radius $1/7$ was chosen to just exceed $\sqrt2/10$.
