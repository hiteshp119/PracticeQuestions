## Problem

Bond A has a 50% chance of defaulting next year; bond B has 30%. What is the range of possible probabilities that at least one defaults? What is the corresponding range of their default correlation?

## Solution

1. $P(A\cup B) = 0.5 + 0.3 - P(AB)$, with $0\le P(AB)\le \min(0.5, 0.3) = 0.3$:
   - $P(AB) = 0$ (never both): $P(A\cup B) = 0.8$;
   - $P(AB) = 0.3$ (B ⊂ A): $P(A\cup B) = 0.5$.
2. Indicators $I_A, I_B$: $\operatorname{Var}(I_A) = 0.5\cdot0.5 = 0.25$, $\operatorname{Var}(I_B) = 0.3\cdot0.7 = 0.21$,
$$\rho = \frac{P(AB) - 0.15}{\sqrt{0.25\cdot0.21}} = \frac{P(AB) - 0.15}{\sqrt{0.0525}}.$$
3. Endpoints: $P(AB) = 0 \Rightarrow \rho = -\frac{0.15}{0.2291} = -\sqrt{3/7}\approx-0.655$; $P(AB) = 0.3\Rightarrow\rho = +\sqrt{3/7}$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Plug $\rho = \pm1$ into $P(A\cup B) = 0.65 - \rho\sqrt{0.0525}$.

**Why it fails.** It gives probabilities outside $[0.5, 0.8]$: two events with different probabilities can't be perfectly correlated.

**The trick.** Parametrize by the joint probability $P(AB)$, whose bounds are obvious (Fréchet bounds), then translate into correlation. For binary variables the attainable correlation range is narrower than $[-1,1]$.
