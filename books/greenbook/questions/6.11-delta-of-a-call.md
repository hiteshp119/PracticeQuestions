## Problem

What is the delta of a European call on a non-dividend-paying stock? Derive it.

## Solution

1. $c = SN(d_1) - Ke^{-rT}N(d_2)$ with $d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r + \frac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T}$, $d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt T$. Both depend on $S$, and $\frac{\partial d_1}{\partial S} = \frac{\partial d_2}{\partial S} = \frac1{S\sigma\sqrt T}$.
2. Chain rule:
$$\begin{aligned}\frac{\partial c}{\partial S} = N(d_1) &+ \Big(SN'(d_1) - Ke^{-rT}N'(d_2)\Big)\\ &\times\frac{1}{S\sigma\sqrt T}.\end{aligned}$$
3. **Key identity.** $d_2^2 = d_1^2 - 2d_1\sigma\sqrt T + \sigma^2T = d_1^2 - 2\ln(S/K) - 2rT$, so
$$\begin{aligned}N'(d_2) &= N'(d_1)\,e^{\ln(S/K) + rT} = N'(d_1)\frac{S}{K}e^{rT}\\ \Rightarrow\; Ke^{-rT}N'(d_2) &= SN'(d_1).\end{aligned}$$
4. The bracket vanishes:
$$\Delta_{\text{call}} = N(d_1),\qquad \Delta_{\text{put}} = N(d_1) - 1.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Treat $N(d_1), N(d_2)$ as constants: $\partial c/\partial S = N(d_1)$.

**Why it fails.** Right answer, wrong reason; an interviewer will ask why the other terms disappear.

**The trick.** The identity $SN'(d_1) = Ke^{-rT}N'(d_2)$ says the two legs of the call are equally sensitive at the exercise boundary. Delta is the hedge ratio: $N(d_1)$ shares per call (and the probability of exercise under the stock measure).
