## Problem

A car is behind one of three doors, goats behind the other two. You pick a door. The host, who knows where the car is, always opens one of the other two doors to show a goat, then offers you the chance to switch to the remaining closed door. Should you switch? What is your probability of winning if you do?

## Solution

1. **Stay:** you win iff your first pick was the car: $\frac13$. The host's action can't change that.
2. **Switch:**
   - first pick = car ($\frac13$): switching loses;
   - first pick = goat ($\frac23$): the host must open the *other* goat, so the remaining door is the car. Switching wins.
3.
$$P(\text{win}\mid\text{switch}) = \tfrac23.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Two doors are left, so it's 50-50."

**Why it fails.** The host's choice is not random: he is *forced* to avoid the car. His action carries information about the other doors, not about yours.

**The trick.** Imagine 100 doors: you pick one, the host opens 98 goats and leaves one other door closed. You'd switch at once. Your door keeps its $\frac1{100}$; the rest of the probability collapses onto the one door the host carefully left shut.
