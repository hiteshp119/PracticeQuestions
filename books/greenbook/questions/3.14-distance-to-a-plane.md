## Problem

Find the distance from the origin to the plane $2x + 3y + 4z = 12$.

## Solution

1. Minimize the squared distance:
$$\begin{aligned}\min\ & f = x^2+y^2+z^2\\ \text{s.t.}\ & g = 2x+3y+4z-12 = 0.\end{aligned}$$
2. Lagrange: $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$:
$$\begin{aligned}(2x,\,2y,\,2z) &= \lambda\,(2,\,3,\,4)\\ \Rightarrow\; (x,y,z) &= \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(2,3,4).\end{aligned}$$
3. Plug into the constraint:
$$\tfrac\lambda2(4+9+16) = 12 \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac\lambda2 = \tfrac{12}{29},$$
$$(x,y,z) = \Big(\tfrac{24}{29},\tfrac{36}{29},\tfrac{48}{29}\Big).$$
4. Distance:
$$D = \tfrac{12}{29}\sqrt{29} = \frac{12}{\sqrt{29}}\approx 2.228.$$

In general, the distance from the origin to $ax+by+cz=d$ is $\dfrac{|d|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}}$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Substitute $x = (12-3y-4z)/2$ and minimize over $y,z$ with partial derivatives.

**Why it fails.** It works but is messy, and it hides the geometry.

**The trick.** The shortest segment to a plane is perpendicular to it, i.e. along the normal $n=(2,3,4)$. Write the foot as $t\,n$, solve $t\,|n|^2 = 12$, and the distance is $t|n| = 12/|n|$. Lagrange's condition is that same statement in algebra.
