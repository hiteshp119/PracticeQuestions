## Problem

Two solid cylinders, each of radius $1$, have axes that cross at a right angle and meet at a point. What is the volume of their intersection?

## Solution

1. Coordinates: axes along $x$ and $y$:
$$\text{cyl}_1: y^2 + z^2 \le 1, \qquad \text{cyl}_2: x^2 + z^2 \le 1.$$
2. Fix height $z\in[-1,1]$. The slice is
$$|x|\le\sqrt{1-z^2},\quad |y|\le\sqrt{1-z^2},$$
a square of side $2\sqrt{1-z^2}$ and area $A(z) = 4(1-z^2)$.
3. Integrate:
$$V = \int_{-1}^{1}4(1-z^2)\,dz = 8\Big[z - \frac{z^3}{3}\Big]_0^1 = \frac{16}{3}.$$
4. For radius $r$: $V = \frac{16}{3}r^3$.

**No-integral version.** The unit sphere fits inside the solid. At every height its slice is the circle inscribed in the square slice, so
$$\frac{A_\text{circle}(z)}{A_\text{square}(z)} = \frac{\pi}{4}\ \ \forall z \;\Rightarrow\; V = \frac{4}{\pi}\cdot\frac43\pi = \frac{16}{3}.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Slice perpendicular to one cylinder's axis: each slice is a circle cut by the other cylinder, an awkward shape.

**Why it fails.** The area of that shape is a messy integral of its own.

**The trick.** Slice along the direction perpendicular to **both** axes. Then each cylinder constrains just one coordinate, and the slice is a square. Comparing with the inscribed sphere (Cavalieri: same area ratio at every height means same volume ratio) skips the integral completely.
