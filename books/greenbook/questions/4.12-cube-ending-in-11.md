## Problem

$x$ is a uniformly random integer from 1 to $10^{12}$. What is the probability that $x^3$ ends in the digits 11?

## Solution

1. Write $x = a + 10b$, $a\in\{0,\dots,9\}$:
$$\begin{aligned}x^3 &= a^3 + 30a^2b + 300ab^2 + 1000b^3\\ &\equiv a^3 + 30a^2 b \pmod{100}.\end{aligned}$$
2. **Units digit.** $a^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{10}$. Cubes of $0..9$ end in $0,1,8,7,4,5,6,3,2,9$, so $a = 1$.
3. **Tens digit.** With $a = 1$: $x^3 \equiv 1 + 30b \pmod{100}$. Need $30b \equiv 10$, i.e. $3b\equiv 1 \pmod{10}$, so $b \equiv 7\pmod{10}$.
4. So $x \equiv 71 \pmod{100}$; check $71^3 = 357{,}911$. ✓ Since $10^{12}$ is a multiple of 100, exactly 1 in 100 integers qualifies:
$$P = 0.01.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Try cubes of many numbers and look for a pattern.

**Why it fails.** Slow, and you can't be sure the pattern is complete.

**The trick.** Peel off digits one at a time (mod 10, then mod 100). Cubing is a bijection on the last digit, and the next digit enters **linearly** ($30a^2b$), so each step has a unique solution.
