## Problem

You have 1000 bottles of wine; exactly one is poisoned. The poison kills a mouse exactly 18 hours after any amount is drunk, with no earlier symptoms. You have 10 mice and 20 hours. How can you identify the poisoned bottle for certain? With your scheme, if mice 6, 7 and 9 die (counting from the lowest bit), which bottle was poisoned?

## Solution

1. Only one round of testing fits in 20 hours, so all mice drink at once. Each mouse's fate is one bit: $10$ mice give $2^{10} = 1024\ge1000$ possible outcomes, just enough.
2. **Scheme.** Label bottles $1..1000$ in 10-bit binary. Mouse $k$ ($k = 1$ is the lowest bit) takes a sip from every bottle whose bit $k$ is 1.
3. After 18 hours, mouse $k$ is dead iff the poisoned bottle's bit $k$ is 1. Reading dead = 1, alive = 0 gives the label.
4. Mice 6, 7, 9 dead (bits $2^5, 2^6, 2^8$):
$$0101100000_2 = 32 + 64 + 256 = 352.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Binary search: split the bottles in halves and test one half at a time.

**Why it fails.** That needs 10 sequential rounds of 18 hours each.

**The trick.** Make the tests **non-adaptive**: design all questions in advance so each test answers one independent bit. It's binary search done in parallel (the same encoding as card 2.29).
