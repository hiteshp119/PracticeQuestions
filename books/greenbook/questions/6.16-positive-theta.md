## Problem

When can a European option have positive theta?

## Solution

1. Theta ($\Theta = \partial V/\partial t$) for a European put, no dividends:
$$\Theta_p = -\frac{SN'(d_1)\sigma}{2\sqrt T} + rKe^{-rT}N(-d_2).$$
2. **Deep ITM put** ($S\ll K$): $N'(d_1)\approx0$, $N(-d_2)\approx1$:
$$\Theta_p\approx rKe^{-rT} > 0.$$
The put is essentially a claim to $K - S$ paid at $T$; as $T$ approaches, the discount on $K$ shrinks. (That's also why an American put may be exercised early.)
3. **Call with dividend yield $y$:**
$$\begin{aligned}\Theta_c = &-\frac{SN'(d_1)\sigma e^{-yT}}{2\sqrt T}\\ &+ ySe^{-yT}N(d_1) - rKe^{-rT}N(d_2).\end{aligned}$$
Deep ITM ($N(d_{1,2})\approx1$, $N'\approx0$) with large $y$: the $ySe^{-yT}$ term can win, so $\Theta_c > 0$.
4. Non-dividend European calls: $\Theta_c = -\frac{SN'(d_1)\sigma}{2\sqrt T} - rKe^{-rT}N(d_2) < 0$ always. American options: more time is never worth less, so $\Theta\le0$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** "Options always lose value as time passes."

**Why it fails.** Two forces: optionality (decays) and the time value of money on $K$ or the dividends (can go either way).

**The trick.** Deep ITM, the option is almost a forward/bond; then theta is the bond's accretion, which is positive for the put holder who is owed $K$.
