## Problem

A player takes 100 free throws. She makes the first and misses the second. For every later throw, her probability of scoring equals the fraction of throws made so far (e.g. 23 made after 40 throws ⇒ $\frac{23}{40}$ on throw 41). What is the probability she makes exactly 50 of the 100?

## Solution

1. Let $P_{n,k} = P(k \text{ made after } n \text{ throws})$. Start: $P_{2,1} = 1$.
2. $n=3$: score with $\frac12$, so $P_{3,1} = P_{3,2} = \frac12$. $n = 4$: $P_{4,1} = \frac12\cdot\frac23 = \frac13$, $P_{4,2} = \frac12\cdot\frac13 + \frac12\cdot\frac13 = \frac13$, $P_{4,3} = \frac13$.
3. **Claim:** $P_{n,k} = \frac1{n-1}$ for $k = 1,\dots,n-1$. Induction step: reach $k$ after $n+1$ throws from $k$ (miss, $\frac{n-k}{n}$) or $k-1$ (hit, $\frac{k-1}{n}$):
$$\begin{aligned}P_{n+1,k} &= \frac{1}{n-1}\Big(\frac{n-k}{n} + \frac{k-1}{n}\Big)\\ &= \frac{1}{n-1}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n} = \frac1n.\end{aligned}$$
(The edge cases $k=1$, $k=n$ work since one of the two terms is 0.)
4. So
$$P_{100,50} = \frac1{99}.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Sum over all orders of 48 hits and 50 misses among throws 3–100 with their path probabilities.

**Why it fails.** Path probabilities differ, so it looks hopeless.

**The trick.** Compute small cases until a pattern appears, then prove it. Here it's a Pólya urn (start with one "hit" ball and one "miss" ball, add a copy of each drawn ball): every path with the same total has the same probability, and the count ends up uniform.
