## Problem

$X_1, X_2$ are i.i.d. uniform on $[0,1]$; $Y = \min(X_1,X_2)$, $Z = \max(X_1, X_2)$. Find $P(Y\ge y\mid Z\le z)$ for $y, z\in[0,1]$, and the correlation of $Y$ and $Z$.

## Solution

1. $P(Z\le z) = z^2$. For $y\le z$: $Y\ge y$ and $Z\le z$ iff both points lie in $[y,z]$, probability $(z-y)^2$:
$$P(Y\ge y\mid Z\le z) = \frac{(z-y)^2}{z^2}\quad(0\le y\le z),$$
and $0$ if $y > z$.
2. Moments (card 4.44, $n = 2$): $E[Y] = \frac13$, $E[Z] = \frac23$; $f_Y = 2(1-y)$, $f_Z = 2z$:
$$\begin{aligned}E[Y^2] &= \tfrac16 &&\Rightarrow\; \operatorname{Var}(Y) = \tfrac16 - \tfrac19 = \tfrac1{18},\\ E[Z^2] &= \tfrac12 &&\Rightarrow\; \operatorname{Var}(Z) = \tfrac12 - \tfrac49 = \tfrac1{18}.\end{aligned}$$
3. $YZ = X_1X_2$ always, so $E[YZ] = \frac14$ and
$$\operatorname{Cov}(Y,Z) = \tfrac14 - \tfrac13\cdot\tfrac23 = \tfrac1{36}.$$
4.
$$\operatorname{corr}(Y,Z) = \frac{1/36}{1/18} = \frac12.$$

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Derive the joint density $f(y,z) = 2$ on $y<z$ and integrate $yz$.

**Why it fails.** It works but takes longer than needed.

**The trick.** Symmetric functions of $(X_1, X_2)$ don't care which is larger: $\min\cdot\max = X_1 X_2$ and $\min + \max = X_1 + X_2$. Equal variances follow from $Y\overset d= 1 - Z$. Positive correlation makes sense: a large min forces a large max.
