## Problem

Explain some basic interest rate models and their differences.

## Solution

1. **Short-rate vs forward-rate models.** Short-rate models specify the instantaneous rate $R(t)$; forward-rate models (Heath–Jarrow–Morton, LIBOR market model) evolve the whole forward curve.
2. **Equilibrium models** (fixed parameters; don't fit today's curve exactly):
   - **Vasicek:** $dR = a(b - R)\,dt + \sigma\,dW$. Mean reverts to $b$ at speed $a$; Gaussian, so rates can go negative; bond prices in closed form.
   - **Cox–Ingersoll–Ross:** $dR = a(b-R)\,dt + \sigma\sqrt R\,dW$. Mean-reverting; volatility shrinks near 0, keeping rates positive (if $2ab\ge\sigma^2$).
3. **No-arbitrage models** (time-dependent drift calibrated to today's term structure):
   - **Ho–Lee:** $dr = \theta(t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW$. Simplest; no mean reversion.
   - **Hull–White:** $dR = a\big(b(t) - R\big)dt + \sigma\,dW$. Vasicek with a time-varying target, fits the curve exactly.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** List the SDEs.

**Why it fails.** The interviewer wants the trade-offs: realism (mean reversion, positivity) vs tractability vs fitting market prices.

**The trick.** Classify on two axes (what's modelled; whether today's curve is an input) and remember one defect each model fixes: CIR fixes Vasicek's negative rates; Hull–White fixes Vasicek's mismatch with today's curve.
