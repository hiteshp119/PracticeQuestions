## Problem

Your computer can only generate random numbers uniform on $(0,1)$. How do you generate random variables with distribution $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$?

## Solution

1. **Scale and shift:** if $Z\sim N(0,1)$, then $X = \mu + \sigma Z\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$. So generate $Z$.
2. **Inverse transform.** If $U\sim U(0,1)$ and $F$ is a continuous CDF, $F^{-1}(U)$ has CDF $F$. For the normal, $N^{-1}$ has no closed form but good rational approximations exist.
3. **Acceptance–rejection.** To sample density $f$ using an easy density $g$ with $f\le Mg$: draw $Y\sim g$, $V\sim U(0,1)$, accept $Y$ if $V\le\frac{f(Y)}{Mg(Y)}$. Accepted values have density $f$; the acceptance rate is $\frac1M$.
   - $f$ = half-normal $\sqrt{\frac2\pi}e^{-y^2/2}$, $g = e^{-y}$ (sample $Y = -\ln U$):
$$\begin{aligned}\frac fg &= \sqrt{\tfrac2\pi}e^{-y^2/2 + y}\le\sqrt{\tfrac{2e}{\pi}} = M\approx1.32,\\ \frac{f}{Mg} &= e^{-(y-1)^2/2}.\end{aligned}$$
   - Accept, then attach a random sign. Acceptance $\frac1M\approx0.76$.
4. **Box–Muller** (exact, no rejection): $Z_1 = \sqrt{-2\ln U_1}\cos(2\pi U_2)$, $Z_2 = \sqrt{-2\ln U_1}\sin(2\pi U_2)$, two independent normals.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Add 12 uniforms and subtract 6 (CLT).

**Why it fails.** Only approximately normal; tails are cut off at $\pm6$.

**The trick.** Two general tools: invert the CDF, or sample from a simpler envelope and thin it. Box–Muller is the polar-coordinates trick from the Gaussian integral (card 3.09) run in reverse.
