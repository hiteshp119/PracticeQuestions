## Problem

You want two cubes whose front faces can show the day of the month, 01 to 31 (single-digit days shown as 01–09). Each face carries one digit and you may swap the cubes' order. What digits go on each cube?

## Solution

1. **11 and 22** need a 1 and a 2 on **both** cubes.
2. **01–09.** If only one cube had a 0, the other cube would need all of $1,\dots,9$ on 6 faces. So **both** cubes need a 0.
3. That's $3+3$ faces used, with 6 faces left for the 7 digits $3,4,5,6,7,8,9$.
4. The 6 and the 9 are never needed at the same time (no day uses both), and a 6 turned upside down is a 9:

| cube | faces |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0 1 2 3 4 5 |
| 2 | 0 1 2 6 7 8 |

Check: every day $d_1d_2$ has $d_1 \in\{0,1,2,3\}$ on one cube and $d_2$ on the other (e.g. 30 = 3 on cube 1, 0 on cube 2; 19 = 1 on cube 1, 6→9 on cube 2).

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Count faces: 12 faces, 10 digits. It looks easy until the forced duplicates eat 6 faces.

**Why it fails.** Counting digits once ignores which digits must appear on *both* cubes.

**The trick.** First list the hard constraints (doubles 11, 22; leading zeros), see that they leave one face too few, then find the trick that saves a face: 6 doubles as 9.
