## Problem

A box has $n$ balls, all of different colours. Each step you pick an ordered pair of distinct balls at random, repaint the first to the colour of the second, and put both back. What is the expected number of steps until all balls have the same colour?

## Solution

1. **Martingale.** The count $X$ of any one colour moves $\pm1$ with equal probabilities $\frac{X(n-X)}{n(n-1)}$, so it's a martingale; a colour with $i$ balls ends up winning with probability $\frac in$.
2. **Condition on colour 1 winning** (by symmetry $E[N] = E[N\mid F_1]$). Bayes with $h(i) = \frac in$:
$$\begin{aligned}P(i\to i\pm1\mid F_1) &= \frac{i(n-i)}{n(n-1)}\cdot\frac{h(i\pm1)}{h(i)}\\ &= \frac{(n-i)(i\pm1)}{n(n-1)}.\end{aligned}$$
The state stays put with the remaining probability $1 - \frac{2i(n-i)}{n(n-1)}$.
3. Let $Z_i$ = expected remaining steps (given $F_1$), $Z_n = 0$:
$$\begin{aligned}\frac{2i(n-i)}{n(n-1)}Z_i = 1 &+ \frac{(n-i)(i+1)}{n(n-1)}Z_{i+1}\\ &+ \frac{(n-i)(i-1)}{n(n-1)}Z_{i-1}.\end{aligned}$$
4. Solving from $i = 1$ up (the $Z_0$ term vanishes) gives
$$Z_1 = (n-1)^2.$$
Checks: $n=2$: one step always ends it, $1 = (2-1)^2$. ✓ $n = 3$: $4$.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Track the full colour configuration.

**Why it fails.** The number of configurations explodes (partitions of $n$).

**The trick.** Symmetry plus conditioning on the winner reduces everything to one number, the count of the eventual winning colour, which drifts upward once you condition on its success. The $(n-1)^2$ is the "random walk to absorb" scaling: about $n^2$ steps to merge $n$ colours.
