## Problem

From a shuffled 52-card deck you turn over two cards at a time. Both black: dealer's pile. Both red: your pile. One of each: discarded. After all 52 cards, you win \$100 if your pile is strictly bigger; otherwise nothing.

How much would you pay to play?

## Solution

Let $d$ = number of mixed (discarded) pairs. Each removes one red and one black card.

$$\text{your pile} = 26 - d = \text{dealer's pile}.$$

The piles always **tie**, and ties go to the house. $P(\text{win}) = 0$, so the fair price is \$0.

## Intuition

**Naive approach.** Model it as a random walk over pairs and try to compute the probability your pile wins.

**Why it fails.** It looks random, but the outcome is fully determined. No probability is needed at all.

**The trick.** Look for what **can't change**: equal numbers of red and black leave the table through discards, so the two piles are always equal.
